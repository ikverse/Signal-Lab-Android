# Derivatives, positioning and liquidation signals for 1-day to 4-week spot returns (as of 2026-10-22)

Scope note for the writer: every "live probe" below is a keyless HTTPS GET/POST I ran on 2026-10-22 from a PC; the responses are quoted, not the docs. Several primary papers were behind 403/PDF walls (SSRN, ScienceDirect, two CMU PDFs); where I could only read a search-engine abstract I say "abstract/snippet-level" and the finding should be weighted accordingly. Fee frame used throughout: 0.25% round trip BTC/ETH, 0.40% other top-50, 0.75% below rank 50.

## Key question 1: Liquidation cascades and forced deleveraging as a buy signal (1 to 10 days)

### Takeaway
No study, vendor note or backtest found reports forward returns after large long-liquidation days at the 1 to 10 day horizon; the only quantified evidence (K33, VanEck) points the other way (weak or negative follow-through), and the two 2026 academic papers on cascades say they are abrupt, shock-driven events with no reliable per-event precursor. Liquidation totals are not available free and keyless from any source; Binance's own stream is a 1-per-second-per-symbol snapshot, the REST aggregate endpoint is gone, and Coinglass has no free API plan.

### Cited Findings
- A July 2026 single-author preprint studied seven BTC perpetual liquidation cascades (2022 to 2025, incl. the ~USD 19bn 10 Oct 2025 event) with minute price data and 5-minute leverage/order-flow data, using rolling variance and lag-1 autocorrelation as early-warning indicators across 39 configurations per variable per event. Price showed "critical slowing down" in 5 of 7 events but was silent in the two sudden tariff shocks; taker order-flow variance compression was the only regularity across events (placebo test, Fisher-combined p ≈ 5e-6) but works only as a population-level precursor, "not a per-event alarm"; no variable was event-invariant. Not peer reviewed. — [arXiv:2607.27070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27070)
- Press coverage of the same paper adds that it used Binance USD-margined BTCUSDT (May 2022 LUNA, Nov 2022 FTX, Aug 2024 yen carry, Dec 2024, Feb 2025, Apr 2025, Oct 2025), that Binance's per-event `liquidationSnapshot` dumps "have been discontinued" so the author used consequences of liquidations (OI, aggressor flow) instead, and that 8-hourly funding is too coarse for intraday warning. Single secondary source; I could not open the article itself (403). — [CryptoSlate via search](https://cryptoslate.com/new-bitcoin-study-shows-the-strongest-recurring-liquidation-warning-signs-cannot-warn-of-an-individual-crash/)
- Part II (Aug 2026) of the same series, using an on-chain fill log from an unnamed "fully transparent venue" for the Oct 2025 crash: 88% of post-onset forced selling lands within 30 minutes, 63% of it is absorbed off-book by the venue backstop, OI clears 25 to 70% during cascades, the branching ratio stays 0.1 to 0.2 ("deeply subcritical"). The abstract reports no post-cascade return, drift or recovery figures and says no scalar pre-state measure grades cascade severity. — [arXiv:2608.03616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03616)
- An SSRN event study of the 10 to 11 Oct 2025 cascade attributes it to tariff news and reports GARCH persistence α+β ≈ 0.90 and contagion ~20% stronger than 2018 trade-war spillovers; it covers only a short window and gives no forward returns. — [SSRN 5611392](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5611392)
- K33 (Nov 2025, via The Block): after perp OI rose by more than 36,000 BTC in a week (largest since Apr 2023) with rising funding during a drawdown, head of research Vetle Lunde said the structure "statistically mirrors seven similar regimes over the past five years, six of which saw continued declines over the following month, with an average 30-day return of -16%"; CME premiums were near yearly lows at the same time. Seven episodes, single firm, no methodology published. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/379464/bitcoin-derivatives-market-forming-dangerous-setup-as-hopes-of-swift-bounce-fuel-rapid-leverage-climb-k33)
- FXStreet (Sept 2025) citing K33 and CryptoQuant: some long-liquidation spikes mark local bottoms but "more often result in weaker performance afterward"; qualitative, no sample or horizon. — [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/whats-next-for-bitcoin-after-spike-in-long-liquidations-202509232105)
- VanEck's mid-August 2026 ChainCheck tracks 12 capitulation signals; with 8 of 12 firing, historical mean 90-day returns were +12.8% and 180-day +32.0%, both below the unconditional baselines of 15.2% and 36.3%; the cohort only beat baseline at one year, and VanEck itself flags a small, heavily overlapping sample. Snippet-level: the page redirected in a loop and I could not read which 12 signals are included. — [VanEck via search](https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/blogs/digital-assets/matthew-sigel-vaneck-mid-august-2026-bitcoin-chaincheck/)
- Binance liquidation data: the `<symbol>@forceOrder` and `!forceOrder@arr` WebSocket streams push, per symbol, "only the latest one liquidation order within 1000ms" (update speed 1000 ms); no stream is sent in a second with no liquidation. — [Binance USDⓈ-M docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/websocket-market-streams/All-Market-Liquidation-Order-Streams)
- Binance derivatives changelog: on 2026-04-10 the stream description changed to "only the largest one liquidation order" per 1000 ms (was "latest"); on 2026-04-06 the signed `forceOrders` endpoints (`/fapi/v1`, `/dapi/v1`, `/papi/v1/um`, `/papi/v1/cm`) were limited to the past 90 days; no entry mentions `allForceOrders`. — [Binance change log](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/change-log)
- Live probe 2026-10-22: `GET https://fapi.binance.com/fapi/v1/allForceOrders?symbol=BTCUSDT&limit=5` returned HTTP 404 (Binance error page). The public market-wide liquidation REST endpoint no longer exists. — my probe, no URL
- Coinglass pricing page lists no free API plan: Hobbyist USD 29/mo (80+ endpoints, 30 req/min, personal use), Startup USD 79/mo (130+, 80 req/min), Standard USD 299/mo (commercial), Professional USD 699/mo; daily-interval history is "all-time" on every plan; intraday depth is tiered (e.g. 1h/2h: 180/360/720 days). — [Coinglass pricing](https://coinglass.com/pricing); pair-level liquidation history path `GET /api/futures/liquidation/history` per a Go SDK for API v4 — [coinglass-go](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/tigusigalpa/coinglass-go)
- Hyperliquid's official `POST https://api.hyperliquid.xyz/info` documents no market-wide or per-user liquidation request type; `userFills` (per wallet, max 2000) and `userFillsByTime` (2000 per call, only the 10,000 most recent) have no documented liquidation field; the only liquidation mention is order status `liquidatedCanceled`. No API key is mentioned. — [Hyperliquid info endpoint](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/info-endpoint)
- Third-party Hyperliquid indexers expose `liquidationFills` streams/queries with `liquidatedUser`, `markPx`, `method` fields (GoldRush WebSocket, no historical snapshot on subscribe; Dwellir `liquidationFillsByTime`, 2000-row cap). These are paid/keyed services, not the public API. — [GoldRush](https://goldrush.dev/docs/api-reference/hyperliquid-websocket/liquidation-fills); [Dwellir](https://www.dwellir.com/docs/hyperliquid/hyperliquid-index/fills/liquidation-fills)

### Inferences
- For a long-only spot app the "buy the liquidation flush" idea has no supporting forward-return evidence and two pieces of contrary evidence (K33 -16% average after leverage build-ups, VanEck capitulation cohort under baseline at 90/180 days). Treat it as untested, not as a candidate.
- Because the Binance stream is a per-second snapshot of the largest order, any liquidation total built on it is a floor estimate whose undercount is worst exactly in cascades; summing the stream cannot reproduce Coinglass-style totals. A free, keyless proxy for "forced deleveraging" is the OI drop from `openInterestHist` combined with a large negative taker buy/sell ratio and a price gap, which is also what the arXiv author fell back on.
- If the owner is willing to pay, Coinglass Hobbyist (USD 29/mo, daily history all-time) is the cheapest way to backtest liquidation-day forward returns before building anything; the result of that backtest is unknown.

### Gaps
- No source gives hit rates or net-of-cost returns for buying 1 to 10 days after a top-decile long-liquidation day on Binance. This would have to be computed in-house (Coinglass daily history, or Binance OI-drop proxy).
- VanEck's 12 capitulation signals and their individual forward returns are not in any page I could read.
- K33's seven "similar regimes" are not listed, so the -16% figure cannot be reproduced.

## Key question 2: Open interest (quadrants, OI/market cap leverage regime, OI flush)

### Takeaway
Still no rigorous forward-return test of OI-by-price quadrants or OI/market-cap thresholds anywhere I could find in 2023 to 2026; the one peer-reviewed cross-sectional futures-factor paper tested basis and momentum, not OI. The only quantified OI claims are vendor or analyst statements (CryptoQuant 180-day MA, K33 OI-jump warning) without published samples. Binance now returns `CMCCirculatingSupply` alongside OI, so OI/market-cap can be computed locally, but history stays 30 days.

### Cited Findings
- The TradingView "Open Interest + Price Quadrants [GBB]" indicator claims the price-down/OI-down long signal "has shown edge in backtesting" and the price-up/OI-down quadrant has not; it is a closed script with no published methodology or statistics. — [TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/script/D5187oQf-Open-Interest-Price-Quadrants-GBB/)
- A blog proposes concrete divergence thresholds (7-day OI change ≥ ±15% against a ≥ ±5% opposite price move; 30-day ±25% vs ±10%) and cites an "estimated leverage ratio" rising from 0.45 to 0.57 in one example; no forward returns. — [Axel Adler Jr](https://axeladlerjr.com/bitcoin-open-interest-price-divergence-patterns/)
- CryptoQuant contributor Darkfost argued Binance OI fell more than 31% from its October peak and that OI slipping below its 180-day moving average "has historically mattered more than the raw leverage level" and "often marked significant bottoms"; single analyst, no counts or returns. — [Cryptorank summary](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/771d8-bitcoin-futures-flush-31-of-open-interest)
- Glassnode, 7 Jan 2026 ("Clearing the Decks"): options OI fell from 579,258 BTC on 25 Dec to 316,472 BTC after the 26 Dec expiry (−45%, "largest options OI reset on record"); aggregate futures OI contracted from cycle highs above USD 50bn and was turning higher; dealers short gamma between 95k and 104k. No historical forward-return statistics for similar deleveraging. — [Glassnode](https://research.glassnode.com/the-week-onchain-week-01-2026/)
- CoinDesk (25 Aug 2026): the move above USD 80,000 was "driven by short covering, not excessive leverage" as futures OI collapsed; commentary, no test. — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/08/25/a-bitcoin-short-squeeze-for-the-ages-as-futures-open-interest-collapses)
- K33 (Nov 2025): a one-week perp OI jump of >36,000 BTC with rising funding during a drawdown preceded 30-day declines in six of seven past cases, average −16% (see Q1). — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/379464/bitcoin-derivatives-market-forming-dangerous-setup-as-hopes-of-swift-bounce-fuel-rapid-leverage-climb-k33)
- Chi, Hao, Hu and Ran, Journal of Futures Markets 43(8) 2023, "An empirical investigation on risk factors in cryptocurrency futures" (2017 to 2021): basis, momentum and basis-momentum factors earn significant excess returns; basis is "the strongest signal predicting cross-sectional differences in cryptocurrency futures returns"; daily factor returns much stronger than weekly, monthly insignificant. OI, volume and funding were not among the tested characteristics. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v43y2023i8p1161-1180.html)
- A 2026 arXiv paper on six Binance perpetuals finds opening-period order imbalance predicts returns over 4 to 12 hours with cross-sectional average out-of-sample R² of 3.37%; predictors are order flow and technicals, not OI. — [arXiv:2607.09426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09426)
- Binance `GET https://fapi.binance.com/futures/data/openInterestHist` (symbol, period 5m…12h,1d; limit default 30, max 500; `startTime`/`endTime`): "only the latest 1 month" of data; weight 0; IP limit 1000 req/5 min; periods with no trades are omitted and not backfilled; response has `sumOpenInterest`, `sumOpenInterestValue`, `CMCCirculatingSupply`, `timestamp`. — [Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Open-Interest-Statistics); `CMCCirculatingSupply` was added 2025-07-02 and the 1000/5min limit set 2023-10-19 — [change log](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/change-log)
- COIN-M `GET https://dapi.binance.com/futures/data/openInterestHist` (pair e.g. BTCUSD; `contractType` optional since 2026-08-14, omitted = aggregated across contract types; same 30-day cap; weight 1; `sumOpenInterestValue` in base asset). Live probe 2026-10-22 returned e.g. `{"contractType":"PERPETUAL","sumOpenInterest":"13959060","sumOpenInterestValue":"17051.29 (BTC)","pair":"BTCUSD"}`. — [Binance COIN-M docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/coin-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Open-Interest-Statistics)
- Hyperliquid `POST /info {"type":"metaAndAssetCtxs"}` returns per-market `openInterest`, `funding`, `premium`, `dayNtlVlm`, `oraclePx`, `markPx`, keyless; verified live. No history endpoint for OI. — [Hyperliquid perpetuals](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/info-endpoint/perpetuals)

### Inferences
- Nothing contradicts the prior conclusion that OI quadrants and OI thresholds have no forward-return test. The strongest OI-adjacent, quantified claim is K33's leverage-build-up warning, which is a short-side or veto idea (do not buy dips when OI jumps while funding rises in a drawdown), not an entry.
- With `CMCCirculatingSupply` in the same row, OI/market-cap for every Binance USDT perp is one division away; but the 30-day cap means the app must keep its own archive from today, so any regime percentile needs months before it is usable.
- OI "flush" is observable (OI drop % over N days from the archive) but its forward-return value is untested; VanEck's capitulation cohort suggests multi-signal capitulation underperforms at 90/180 days, which is weak evidence against a flush-buy rule.

### Gaps
- No rigorous study of OI/market-cap or OI/spot-volume as a leverage regime with forward returns found for 2023 to 2026.
- CryptoQuant's 180-day-MA claim has no published instance list.

## Key question 3: Basis and term structure (quarterly premium, BIS crypto carry, contango/backwardation veto)

### Takeaway
Basis is the one derivatives variable with peer-reviewed predictive evidence: the BIS "Crypto carry" paper finds high carry predicts future crashes (horizon and statistics not in the pages I could read), and a 2023 JFM paper finds basis the strongest cross-sectional predictor of crypto futures returns on 2017 to 2021 data. Both are pre-ETF and about futures or cash-and-carry, not long-only spot; the natural use for this app is a veto/regime (very high annualised basis = crowded, crash-prone), and Binance serves a keyless annualised basis for perps and quarterlies, 30 days deep.

### Cited Findings
- BIS Working Paper 1087 "Crypto carry" (Schmeling, Schrimpf, Todorov; Apr 2023; also CEPR DP 20719, 2025): Bitcoin and Ethereum futures and spot on crypto-native and traditional exchanges; carry "averages above 10% annually, and can reach up to 60% per year", far above equities, bonds, FX, commodities; interest differentials explain very little; variation is driven by a volatile convenience yield from smaller trend-chasing investors seeking leveraged upside plus scarce arbitrage capital; "high carry predicts future price crashes" and rising carry coincides with rising prices of crash-risk insurance; cash-and-carry is risky because margins spike and positions are liquidated in drawdowns. The summary page gives no horizon, coefficients or R² for the crash prediction. — [BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.pdf); [CEPR DP20719](https://cepr.org/publications/dp20719)
- Chi, Hao, Hu, Ran (JFM 2023, data 2017 to 2021): basis is the strongest cross-sectional predictor of crypto futures returns; daily factor returns far stronger than weekly; monthly insignificant; momentum premium weak; basis-momentum subsumed by basis. Direction of the basis premium, coin count, exchanges, Sharpe and costs are not in the abstract. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v43y2023i8p1161-1180.html)
- Christin, Routledge, Soska, Zetlin-Jones, "The Crypto Carry Trade" (Aug 2022 draft): shorting futures hedged with long spot "is unusually profitable producing in-sample annual Sharpe ratios in the range of 7 to 10" on the most-traded contracts of one large exchange. Snippet-level; both PDFs I fetched were unreadable binaries. — [CMU PDF](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/azj/files/CarryTrade.v1.0.pdf)
- Borri, Liu, Tsyvinski, Wu, "Cryptocurrency as an Investable Asset Class: Coming of Age" (arXiv Oct 2025, v4 Mar 2026): per the search snippet, "over the full sample, which goes from 2020 to 2025, the annualized Sharpe ratio of the cryptocurrency carry is 6.45"; the abstract page I read does not define the carry construction. — [arXiv:2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14435)
- CF Benchmarks (Oct 2025) argues the CME basis is "primarily driven by price momentum and market sentiment" (positive basis in aggressive expansions, negative in declines) and that ETFs made basis trades scalable; a worked delta-neutral example yields 9.43% annualised on 10% basis and ~7.3% borrow. Contemporaneous description, not a forecast test. — [CF Benchmarks](https://blog.cfbenchmarks.com/revisiting-the-bitcoin-basis-how-momentum-sentiment-impact-the-structural-drivers-of-basis-activity)
- CoinDesk (3 Dec 2025): CME annualised basis fell to −2.35%, deepest backwardation since the FTX collapse; framed as a possible bottom but "backwardation does not automatically imply a bullish inflection". — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2025/12/03/bitcoin-futures-return-to-deepest-backwardation-since-ftx-collapse)
- K33 (Nov 2025): CME futures premiums near yearly lows read as "persistent institutional risk aversion"; Lunde: "such divergence has historically preceded negative price action". Unquantified. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/379464/bitcoin-derivatives-market-forming-dangerous-setup-as-hopes-of-swift-bounce-fuel-rapid-leverage-climb-k33)
- Glassnode's 3-month annualised basis read 2.572% on 6 Mar 2026; Glassnode notes periods where perp basis trades below 3M basis "have historically occurred after a downside price action and de-risking". — [Glassnode Studio](https://studio.glassnode.com/charts/derivatives.FuturesAnnualizedBasis3M?a=BTC)
- Binance `GET https://fapi.binance.com/futures/data/basis` (pair, `contractType` PERPETUAL | CURRENT_QUARTER | NEXT_QUARTER, period, limit ≤ 500, default 30): latest 30 days only; weight 0; fields `indexPrice`, `futuresPrice`, `basis`, `basisRate`, `annualizedBasisRate`. Added 2023-11-01. Live probe 2026-10-22, BTCUSDT CURRENT_QUARTER 1d: `annualizedBasisRate` 0.0518, 0.0542, 0.0594 (5.2 to 5.9%) on the three latest days. — [Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Taker-BuySell-Volume) (page lists basis spec); [change log](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/change-log)
- Deribit `public/get_book_summary_by_currency?currency=BTC&kind=future` returns every BTC future's `mark_price`, `estimated_delivery_price`, `open_interest` (USD for inverse futures) in one keyless call, enough to build a Deribit term structure. — [Deribit docs](https://docs.deribit.com/api-reference/market-data/public-get_book_summary_by_currency.md)

### Inferences
- The BIS result is the single best-grounded "veto" in this whole brief: when annualised quarterly basis is historically high (the paper's regime of crowded leveraged longs), crash risk is elevated. For the app this is a long-side veto or position-size cut, not an entry. The threshold and horizon must be estimated in-house because the readable BIS summary gives neither.
- Backwardation as a contrarian long entry has only anecdote (CoinDesk Dec 2025); BIS's mechanism (retail trend-chasing) is symmetric in spirit, but no paper tests deep negative basis as a long signal. Untested.
- CME basis as a distinct signal from Binance basis needs CME prices; I found no free, keyless CME futures price feed. Binance quarterly basis is a workable stand-in and is available today; CME basis would require a paid source (Glassnode, CF Benchmarks, Amberdata).
- Chi et al.'s cross-sectional basis factor is a long-short futures result at daily horizon on pre-2022 data; it cannot be run long-only on spot and its daily-horizon strength would be heavily eroded by the 0.40 to 0.75% round-trip frame.

### Gaps
- BIS crash-prediction horizon, coefficients and sample dates: not readable from the summary page; the 54-page PDF needs a local PDF reader (the fetched bytes were saved to the tool-results folder as a binary).
- Direction and size of Chi et al.'s basis premium; whether the sign is "high basis → high futures return" (carry-like) or the reverse.
- No post-2024 (ETF era) replication of either basis result found.

## Key question 4: Options (DVOL, 25-delta skew, put/call OI, max pain, variance risk premium)

### Takeaway
Deribit's public API is free, keyless and serves DVOL daily candles back to 2021-03-24 plus a one-call snapshot of every option's OI and mark IV (so skew and put/call OI are computable but not historical). Evidence that options data predicts direction at 1 day to 4 weeks is thin: one SSRN abstract says volatility-of-volatility predicts BTC excess returns, one 2026 journal abstract finds a priced volatility-beta spread at weekly horizon, and the only skew backtest I could read (Tradewize, 2021 to 2026) finds extreme skew marks turns more than continuations with no significance tests and no baseline. Max pain has no evidence beyond anecdote.

### Cited Findings
- Almeida, Grith, Miftachov, Wang, "Risk Premia in the Bitcoin Market" (arXiv v2, Aug 2025): Bitcoin "is much more volatile and has a higher variance risk premium than the S&P 500"; two option-implied volatility regimes; in the low-volatility regime the Bitcoin VRP is high and more of the return premium comes from positive returns, in the high-volatility regime the VRP is lower and premia are balanced. No return-predictability test in the abstract. — [arXiv:2410.15195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15195)
- Atanasova, Miao, Segarra, Willeboordse, "What Do Crypto Options Tell Us? Risk Premia Implied by BTC Option Prices" (SSRN): per the search abstract, uses Deribit BTC options to build variance-risk-premium and volatility-of-volatility (VoV) measures and finds "option-implied factors — particularly VoV — predict BTC excess returns". SSRN returned 403; horizon, R² and out-of-sample status unknown; single source. — [SSRN 6771170](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6771170)
- "The price of crypto-market volatility risk" (ScienceDirect, 2026): cryptocurrencies with intermediate crypto-market volatility beta earn a 5.221% higher five-factor-adjusted weekly return, implying a negative volatility risk premium for extreme positive volatility beta. Snippet-level (403). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0275531926001017)
- Winkel and Härdle (2023), Deribit pricing kernels: U-shaped for short-dated, W-shaped for long-dated options; investors "pay a substantial risk premium to insure themselves against short-term price movements". — [HU Berlin edoc](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/18452/27204)
- Tradewize skew study (Deribit daily closes, 2021-01-01 to 2026-09-16; 5,576 BTC and 3,674 ETH instruments; skew = 30-day 25-delta put IV minus call IV from last-trade prices): BTC was put-skewed on 45% of 2,085 days (median −0.9 pts); when skew was above +10 (174 days) the next 30 days were up 63% of the time, mean +2.9%; below −10, up 55%, mean +6.4% (mostly 2021); seven of the twelve biggest one-day falls started from a call-skewed market; daily skew change vs daily return rank correlation −0.29 (skew lags price); median put/call volume ratio 0.66 and the ratio "follows the skew regime rather than leading it"; regimes (60-day median sign held ≥ 45 days) ran 73 to 948 days. No baseline up-rate, no significance tests, overlapping windows, no costs. — [Tradewize](https://tradewize.io/blog/how-to-read-options-skew)
- Deribit Insights "Finding edge in four years of volatility regimes" defines skew as 1-month call IV minus put IV and z-scores it; the page returned 503 so its results are unread. — [Deribit Insights](https://insights.deribit.com/industry/bitcoin-options-finding-edge-in-four-years-of-volatility-regimes/)
- Max pain: Decrypt (27 Aug 2026) notes large expiries "don't automatically move price", citing a USD 15bn June 2025 expiry (max pain 102k) and a USD 13.3bn December expiry with muted reactions; the 28 Aug 2026 expiry had 81,700 BTC options, put/call 0.83, max pain 9 to 11k below spot. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/376752). KuCoin's pro-max-pain blog is promotional with no sample. — [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/en-bitcoin-options-max-pain-price-how-expiring-options-shape-btc-short-term-trends). A MEXC news item reports Deribit saying recent quarterly expiries "have not consistently demonstrated a strong pinning effect"; I could not verify the original statement. — [MEXC](https://www.mexc.com/news/867786)
- Glassnode (19 Oct 2026): BTC 25-delta skew rose from 5.2% to 8.6% in a week, still below the top of its historical range; Glassnode's skew is normalised by ATM IV. — [Glassnode Studio](https://studio.glassnode.com/charts/derivatives.Options25DeltaSkewAll?a=BTC&e=deribit)
- VanEck (Apr 2026): put premiums relative to spot volume more than six times their April 2024 level; no forward return stats. — [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/vaneck-flags-dual-bullish-for-bitcoin)
- Deribit `public/get_volatility_index_data` (currency BTC | ETH | USDC | USDT | EURR; `start_timestamp`, `end_timestamp` ms; `resolution` "1" | "60" | "3600" | "43200" | "1D"): public, `security: []`; returns `[ts, open, high, low, close]` candles plus a `continuation` cursor. — [Deribit docs](https://docs.deribit.com/api-reference/market-data/public-get_volatility_index_data.md). Live probe 2026-10-22 on `https://www.deribit.com/api/v2/...&resolution=1D` from 2019: first BTC candle 1616544000000 = 2021-03-24 (open 84.8, close 95.04); first ETH candle same date (94.18 / 106.73); latest BTC close 45.13 on 2026-10-22. — my probe
- Deribit `public/get_book_summary_by_currency?currency=BTC&kind=option`: one keyless call returns every BTC option with `open_interest` (in BTC for options), `mark_iv`, `underlying_price`, `underlying_index`, `volume`, `volume_usd`, bid/ask/mark. Live probe returned e.g. `BTC-27NOV26-73000-P open_interest 240 mark_iv 41.95 underlying_price 84891.99`. Docs recommend WebSocket `ticker.{instrument}` over polling. No history. — [Deribit docs](https://docs.deribit.com/api-reference/market-data/public-get_book_summary_by_currency.md)
- Deribit rate limits: non-matching-engine requests cost 500 credits from a 50,000 pool refilling at 10,000/s (≈ 20 req/s sustained, burst 100); `public/get_instruments` 1 req/s burst 50; exceeding returns error 10028. — [Deribit rate limits](https://docs.deribit.com/articles/rate-limits)
- Third-party DVOL/skew history: Amberdata (paid) DVOL from 2021-05-01 — [Amberdata docs](https://docs.amberdata.io/docs/analytics/derivatives/volatility-index); Glassnode (paid) DVOL OHLC and 25-delta skew — [Glassnode](https://studio.glassnode.com/charts/derivatives.DvolOhlcPit?a=BTC); oanor.com advertises a keyless DVOL history and Deribit-order-book skew API (unverified reliability, paid call tiers) — [oanor](https://oanor.com/tag/dvol)

### Inferences
- DVOL is the only options series the app can backfill for free today (5.5 years daily, keyless, one paginated call). Skew and put/call OI must be snapshotted daily from `get_book_summary_by_currency` from now on; a 25-delta skew needs strike interpolation per expiry, which is doable from `mark_iv` and `underlying_price`.
- The literature supports DVOL as a regime variable (VRP is high and upside-tilted in the low-vol regime per Almeida et al.), not as a 1 to 10 day directional signal. The one directional claim (VoV, Atanasova et al.) is unverified and single-source.
- Tradewize's skew extremes: +2.9% mean 30-day return after skew > +10 on 174 overlapping days is within noise without a baseline; with 0.25% round trip it would be gross-positive but statistically unsupported. Not actionable.
- Max pain: no evidence; skip.

### Gaps
- Horizon, R² and out-of-sample status of the VoV result; whether it uses DVOL or a Deribit-surface VoV.
- Any study of DVOL (or IV minus realised vol) predicting realised vol at 1 to 4 weeks specifically for sizing; the Deribit Insights piece likely has this but was unreachable.
- Deribit's own put/call OI ratio history is not exposed by any public endpoint I found.

## Key question 5: CME weekend gap fills

### Takeaway
The best control-group test (427 weekends, Dec 2017 to May 2026) finds gaps fill at the same rate as an arbitrary level the same distance away (80% vs 83% within a week), a fade loses more races than it wins before fees, and CME moved to near-24/7 trading on 29 May 2026 so new weekend gaps no longer form. Not tradable, and moot going forward.

### Cited Findings
- Tradewize: 427 CME weekends Dec 2017 to May 2026, gap = Friday 4pm Chicago close to Sunday 5pm reopen, measured on Binance spot BTCUSDT hourly at CME times, cross-checked against CME daily bars (gap-size correlation 0.90). Fill rates 22% within an hour, 55% within a day, 80% within a week, 89% within a month, 97% eventually; a made-up level the same distance on the other side was reached 30% / 65% / 83% / 90% / 99%. The made-up level was reached first on 231 weekends vs 161 for the gap (35 ties); gaps of 3 to 5% (56 weekends) favoured the gap 33 to 23 but ≥ 5% gaps (43) favoured the control 29 to 14. A fade (short at reopen, target Friday price, symmetric stop) "lost more races than it won (161 to 231)" before fees. CME went 24/7 on 29 May 2026; 14 of 16 gaps open at the switch remained. — [Tradewize](https://tradewize.io/blog/how-to-read-the-cme-gap)
- Markets Science (older study, BitMEX data): CME gaps "eventually filled over 95% of the time since December 2017" and a fade-and-wait strategy was "marginally profitable at best". The page is image-based and I could not read the table. — [Markets Science](https://marketsscience.com/gap_study.html)
- CoinDesk Research (Mar 2025, via search): 79 of the previous 80 CME gaps had filled. — [via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/bitcoin-first-cme-gap-free-174649486.html)
- CME crypto futures trade 24/7 from Friday 29 May 2026 4:00 pm CT, with a 2-minute daily pause at 4:00 pm CT and a 2-hour Saturday 2:00 to 4:00 am CT maintenance window; weekend trades carry the next business day's trade date. Broker pages, not a CME primary source. — [Optimus Futures](https://www.optimusfutures.com/Bitcoin.php); [Fintool](https://fintool.com/news/cme-24-7-crypto-futures-trading)

### Inferences
- Unconditional fill rates (80 to 97%) are a volatility artefact, as the control shows; there is no edge net of even the 0.25% BTC round trip, and the 3 to 5% band (71% vs 52% within a week, n = 56) is too small to act on.
- Since no new gaps form after May 2026, the only residual use is the 14 still-open historical levels as reference prices, which is lore, not signal.

### Gaps
- No CME primary-source confirmation of the 24/7 launch date was retrieved (broker pages only).

## Key question 6: Binance positioning ratios (top-trader L/S accounts and positions, global L/S accounts, taker buy/sell)

### Takeaway
Still no forward-return test of any Binance long/short or taker ratio anywhere in 2023 to 2026; only daily commentary that explicitly calls them "not a clean signal". The only peer-reviewed positioning evidence in crypto uses CFTC Commitments of Traders for CME Bitcoin (weekly): leveraged funds show market-timing via short adjustments (Baur and Smales 2022) and speculative net-short changes predict returns (Dunbar and Owusu-Amoako 2023). Binance serves 30 days of all four ratios; the CFTC file is free, keyless and carries CME Bitcoin, Micro Bitcoin and Coinbase nano contracts.

### Cited Findings
- Binance `GET /futures/data/topLongShortPositionRatio` and `topLongShortAccountRatio` (top 20% of users by margin balance): limit default 30, max 500; "Only the data of the latest 30 days is available"; IP limit 1000 req/5 min. — [Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Top-Trader-Long-Short-Ratio); [accounts](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Top-Long-Short-Account-Ratio)
- `GET /futures/data/globalLongShortAccountRatio` (all traders, by account count): periods 5m…12h,1d; latest 30 days; weight 0; fields `longShortRatio`, `longAccount`, `shortAccount`. — [Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Long-Short-Ratio)
- `GET /futures/data/takerlongshortRatio`: live probe 2026-10-22 returned `{"buySellRatio":"1.0212","sellVol":"47906.378","buyVol":"48924.296","timestamp":1792281600000}` for BTCUSDT 1d; a request with `startTime` ~90 days back returned `{"msg":"parameter 'startTime' is invalid.","code":-1130}`, confirming the 30-day cap is enforced on the taker endpoint too. Rate limit 1000/5 min set 2023-10-19. — my probe; [change log](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/change-log)
- Live probe 2026-10-22, BTCUSDT 1d: topLongShortPositionRatio 1.8258 and 1.8428 (longAccount 0.646/0.648); globalLongShortAccountRatio 1.6567 and 1.4969. — my probe
- Blockchain.news daily reports using these ratios state the cohort divergence "does not resolve into a clean directional signal and should not be extrapolated beyond the platform". — [Blockchain.News](https://blockchain.news/news/20261020-price-prediction-bnb-sell-side-pressure-caps-near-term)
- Baur and Smales, "Trading behavior in bitcoin futures: Following the 'smart money'", Journal of Futures Markets 42(7) 2022, CFTC COT: leveraged money traders hold the largest positions, are typically net short, "display market timing ability" mostly by adjusting shorts, other trader types follow in later periods, and "profitable strategies can be constructed" from changes in leveraged money positions. Sample period, horizon and statistics not in the abstract. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v42y2022i7p1304-1323.html)
- Dunbar and Owusu-Amoako, "Predictability of crypto returns: The impact of trading behavior", Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Finance 39 (2023): "net-short trading behavior of speculative retail traders is an economically strong and statistically significant determinant of cryptocurrency returns", robust to investor attention, crypto uncertainty, sentiment and prior returns; sign, sample, horizon and out-of-sample results not in the abstract (keyword "Bitcoin futures"). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/beexfi/v39y2023ics2214635023000266.html); companion paper finds commercial hedgers' net position predicts out of sample — [FRL 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612323003811)
- A related paper reports that "trading activity and extreme sentiment of speculators and retailers present significant predicting power on the subsequent bitcoin futures price changes over different time horizons" (abstract only). — [XJTLU](https://scholar.xjtlu.edu.cn/en/publications/bitcoin-futures-risk-premia/)
- CFTC COT is published Fridays 15:30 ET for the preceding Tuesday; Traders in Financial Futures current-week file `https://www.cftc.gov/dea/newcot/FinFutWk.txt` (futures-only) and `FinComWk.txt` (combined); historical compressed files at `/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/HistoricalCompressed/index.htm`. — [CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm). Live probe 2026-10-22: the file contains rows `"BITCOIN - CHICAGO MERCANTILE EXCHANGE"` (code 133741, as of 2026-10-13, OI 20,752), `"MICRO BITCOIN - CHICAGO MERCANTILE EXCHANGE"` (133742), and Coinbase Derivatives `"Nano Bitcoin"` and `"NANO BITCOIN PERP STYLE"` rows. — my probe

### Inferences
- The Binance ratios remain untested; the 30-day cap means any test needs the app's own archive, which it already keeps for OI and could extend to the four ratios at zero weight cost.
- The CFTC evidence is weekly and 2017 to ~2022 vintage, so at best a slow regime input (leveraged-funds net short change) for a daily app; both papers' abstracts omit the sign, so even the direction has to be re-estimated before use. Coinbase Derivatives' "perp style" rows are new and have no literature.
- Because leveraged funds on CME are structurally net short as the basis-trade leg (CF Benchmarks), their positioning largely mirrors the basis; it is unlikely to add independent information beyond Q3's basis veto.

### Gaps
- No forward-return test for taker buy/sell ratio or any L/S ratio at daily or 4h horizon; no study even of the 30-day windows.
- Sign, sample and horizon of the two CFTC papers (full texts paywalled).

## Key question 7: Funding as a regime (sustained negative funding, reset to zero, dispersion, cross-sectional carry)

### Takeaway
Two independent desks (K33, VanEck) report that sustained negative 30-day-average funding is followed by well above-random 30 to 360 day returns (K33: win rates 83 to 96% vs 55 to 70% random; VanEck: 30-day +11.5% vs +4.5%, 77% hit rate, since 2020), but K33 counted only about seven episodes through 2024 and neither publishes methodology; this is the strongest long-side regime claim in the brief and is gross, multi-week and small-sample. A funding "reset to zero after a long positive stretch" has no evidence at all. The academic "crypto carry" is time-series cash-and-carry (short perp, long spot), not a cross-sectional funding rank; the one cross-sectional test found no alpha at 8-hour horizon.

### Cited Findings
- K33 (report 10 Sept 2024, via The Block): 30-day average funding went negative "for just the seventh time since 2018"; after prior flips, the average 90-day return was 79% and the median 55%. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/315831/perp-signal-bitcoin-bottom-bullish-year-end-k33)
- K33 (6 May 2026, via The Block): 30-day average funding negative for 67 consecutive days, longest this decade (previous comparable run 15 Mar to 16 May 2020); buying in negative-funding regimes had win rates of 83 to 96% over 30 to 360 day holds vs 55 to 70% for random dates (Oct 2018 to present), average and median returns 1.84x to 6.27x random buying, lower average max drawdown and shorter time underwater; Lunde: "sustained periods of negative funding have often occurred near market bottoms". Episode count not stated. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/400182/bitcoin-longest-negative-funding-streak-this-decade-k33-flags-short-squeeze-risk)
- VanEck (24 Apr 2026, via Bitcoin Magazine): since 2020, 30-day returns during negative funding averaged 11.5% vs 4.5% across all periods, 77% positive; when annualised funding was below −5%, subsequent 30-day returns averaged 19.4% and 180-day returns "reached 70%"; 19 of the top 50 180-day return windows since 2020 began on negative-funding days although those are only ~13.6% of the sample; 7-day funding was −1.8%, 10th percentile since late 2020; the only losing 180-day window began June 2022 (per the search summary). — [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/vaneck-flags-dual-bullish-for-bitcoin); [VanEck](https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/blogs/digital-assets/matthew-sigel-vaneck-mid-april-2026-bitcoin-chaincheck/)
- BitMEX 2025 Q3 derivatives report (press release): funding rates were positive 92% of the time, "revealing a structural market bias". Snippet-level; the report URL returned 404. — [DL News press release](https://www.dlnews.com/external/bitmex-study-finds-cryptocurrency-funding-rates-positive-92-of-the-time-revealing-a-structural-market-bias)
- Azka Fayez Junior, "Failure of Cross-Sectional Alpha Screening on Cryptocurrency Perpetual Futures" (SSRN 2026): long-short cross-sectional portfolios on ten Binance USDT perps from OHLCV and funding-rate signals; "the evidence compels rejection of the hypothesis that the employed OHLCV and funding rate signals contain exploitable cross-sectional alpha for large-cap crypto perpetual futures at an eight-hour horizon". Abstract-level (403). — [SSRN 6701738](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6701738)
- He, Manela, Ross, von Wachter, "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures" (arXiv v6, Aug 2024): BTC, ETH, BNB, DOGE, ADA on Binance at hourly frequency, 2020 to 11 Mar 2024; mean absolute deviation from no-arbitrage 60 to 90% per year, deviations comove across coins and shrink ~11% per year; past 120-day spot returns are positively and significantly related to the futures premium (contemporaneous regression, R² ≈ 0.33); the paper contains no regression of future spot returns on funding. — [arXiv:2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888v5)
- Zhivkov (2026), "The Two-Tiered Structure of Cryptocurrency Funding Rate Markets": CEX funding tightly integrated, DEX fragmented, CEX leads price discovery (eight-day snapshot); an MDPI follow-up on 26 exchanges and 812 symbols (Nov 2025 to Jan 2026) finds the structure persists "but varies substantially in magnitude" with no discrete regime shifts. Cross-venue dispersion, not cross-coin; both abstract-level. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/399936354_The_Two-Tiered_Structure_of_Cryptocurrency_Funding_Rate_Markets); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7072/14/5/103)
- Cross-sectional crypto factor literature (Liu-Tsyvinski-Wu common risk factors; Borri-Shakhnov cross-section; JFQA trend factor) sorts on size, momentum, trend and volume, not funding. — [NBER w25882](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25882/w25882.pdf); [JFQA trend factor](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/4C1509ACBA33D5DCAF0AC24379148178/S0022109024000747a.pdf/trend_factor_for_the_cross_section_of_cryptocurrency_returns.pdf)
- Data beyond Binance (the app already stores Binance funding since 2019): Hyperliquid `POST /info {"type":"fundingHistory","coin":"BTC","startTime":...}` is keyless and returned records from 1683849600048 = 12 May 2023 (fields `fundingRate`, `premium`, `time`); `{"type":"predictedFundings"}` returns, per coin, predicted funding on BinPerp, HlPerp, BybitPerp in one call. — [Hyperliquid perpetuals](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/info-endpoint/perpetuals); my probe. Deribit `public/get_funding_rate_history?instrument_name=BTC-PERPETUAL` is public (hourly `interest_8h`, `interest_1h`); verified live. — [Deribit](https://docs.deribit.com/api-reference/market-data/public-get_volatility_index_data.md) (sibling endpoint family)
- Binance `GET /fapi/v1/premiumIndex?symbol=BTCUSDT` returned `lastFundingRate 0.00000678`, `interestRate 0.0001`, `markPrice`, `indexPrice` live. — my probe

### Inferences
- The negative-30-day-funding regime is the one long-side derivatives signal with two independent (if non-academic) quantifications, and its stated 30-day edge (+7 pp over baseline per VanEck) dwarfs the 0.25% BTC round trip. Its weaknesses are the sample (roughly 7 to 9 non-overlapping episodes since 2018, with 2020, 2022 and 2026 dominating) and that neither desk conditions on price trend, so "most hated rally" (negative funding while price rises) is untested as a distinct case. The app already has the data to test this itself on BTC, ETH and alts.
- As a filter for an app that trades alts long-only: funding regime is almost entirely a BTC/ETH story in the sources; alt funding dispersion has no evidence either way.
- Cross-sectional funding carry (long low-funding, short high-funding) needs perps on the short leg and the only direct test (10 coins, 8h horizon) found nothing; it is an extension to flag, not a plan item.

### Gaps
- Number of episodes behind K33's 2026 win rates; whether windows overlap; whether alts were included.
- Any test of "funding returning to zero after a long positive stretch" or of funding dispersion across alts.
- No test of negative funding conditioned on an uptrend.
