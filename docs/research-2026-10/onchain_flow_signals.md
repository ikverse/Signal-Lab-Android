# On-chain, exchange-flow, premium and ETF-flow signals for 1-day to 4-week crypto spot returns, and their free data sources (as of 2026-10-22)

Scope note for the report writer. Everything below was gathered on 2026-10-22. Web search quota ran out partway through, so a handful of items rest on search snippets rather than fetched full texts; each such item is marked "(snippet only)". All API behaviour marked "(probed)" was verified by live HTTPS calls on 2026-10-22 from a PC (curl), not from a phone, but every endpoint is plain HTTPS GET with no key. Fee frame used throughout: 0.25% round trip BTC/ETH, 0.40% other top-50 coins; the app is long-only Binance spot, decides on the UTC daily close, enters at the next day's open.

A cross-cutting data-timing fact that matters for every daily on-chain signal: Coin Metrics' community feed stamped its 2026-10-20 daily rows as available at 02:19–03:24 UTC on 2026-10-21 (probed). A decision taken at the 00:00 UTC close of day D therefore cannot use day D's on-chain row; it must use D−1. bitcoin-data.com's free valuation metrics (MVRV, SOPR, NUPL) are additionally embargoed for 7 days (probed). Blockchain.com's chart series lagged 1–2 days (probed). Only exchange tickers (Coinbase, Upbit), ETF flow tables (published after the US close, i.e. before the UTC daily close) and DefiLlama's stablecoin totals are fresh enough to use on the same day's close.

## 1. Exchange net flows and exchange reserves: predictive power at 1–30 days, and free sources

### Takeaway
The only peer-reviewed daily-frequency evidence (Hoang & Baur, CryptoQuant data 2016–2021) says rising exchange reserves predict lower BTC returns "for all horizons", but publishes no magnitudes, no strategy and no costs; the most recent study (Chi, Chu & Hao 2025, 2017–2023) finds BTC net inflows have essentially no return-forecasting power (adjusted R² 0.001–0.002) and the only daily/weekly results it reports are for volatility and are insignificant. Free daily exchange-flow data exists only for BTC and ETH, from Coin Metrics' keyless community API; CryptoQuant and Glassnode do not expose flows on a free API.

### Cited Findings
- Hoang & Baur ("Effects of Bitcoin Exchange Reserves on Bitcoin Returns and Volatility", SSRN 3902504; published as "Loaded for bear: Bitcoin private wallets, exchange reserves and prices", Journal of Banking & Finance 144, 2022): CryptoQuant reserves for 20 exchanges, 1,979 trading days 2016-01-01 to 2021-06-01, daily. The daily change in exchange reserves (ΔReserve) is "negative and statistically significant for all horizons" for contemporaneous and future returns; long-horizon positive returns associate only with reserve decreases; absolute reserve changes raise volatility, inflows more than outflows; a large negative return is followed by higher reserves the next day (panic deposits). The summary reports no coefficient magnitudes, no R², no trading strategy, no costs, and no out-of-sample split. Exchange reserves averaged about 12% of circulating BTC. — [Quantpedia summary of SSRN 3902504](https://quantpedia.com/bitcoin-returns-and-volatility-predicted-by-bitcoin-exchange-reserves/); JBF citation via [Chi, Chu & Hao 2025, arXiv 2411.06327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06327)
- Chi, Chu & Hao, "Return and Volatility Forecasting Using On-Chain Flows in Cryptocurrency Markets" (arXiv 2411.06327v2, June 2025): sample 2017-12-16 to 2023-01-20; net inflow = wallet-to-exchange inflows minus exchange-to-wallet outflows, aggregated across a named list of exchanges (BiBox, BigONE, Binance, Bitfinex, Bitget, Bithumb, BitMEX, Bitstamp, Bittrex, Bybit, CheckSig, Cobinhood, Coinbase, Coincheck, CoinEx, Crypto.com, Deribit, Gate.io, Gemini, HitBTC and others); intervals 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 hours. "BTC net inflows generally lack predictive power for BTC returns (except at 4 hours)"; the 4-hour coefficient is positive, i.e. inflows precede slightly higher BTC returns, the opposite sign of the Hoang & Baur daily result. Reported adjusted R² for the BTC-on-BTC return regressions are 0.001–0.002; the 2-hour BTC net-inflow coefficient is 0.0053 with t = 0.208 (insignificant). "USDT net inflows into the exchanges, positively predicts BTC and ETH returns at multiple intervals" (2-hour coefficient on USDT net inflow predicting BTC return 6.3×10⁻⁶, t = 4.196, adjusted R² 0.002). ETH net inflows negatively predict ETH returns at all intraday intervals; "US$ 1 million of ETH net inflows predict −1.70% of ETH return in the next hour" (double-variable model, Table 2 Panel B). Inflows are informative, outflows are not: "net outflows do not display a symmetric or consistent relationship". The only daily and weekly regressions reported (Appendix A) are for volatility and the USDT-on-BTC daily volatility coefficient is −6.5×10⁻⁶ with t = −0.088. The paper says it runs "in-sample and out-of-sample regression tests" but no out-of-sample forecast statistic appears in the text extracted. — [arXiv 2411.06327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06327), [PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.06327v2)
- Palazzi, Raimundo Júnior & Klotzle, "From Network Fundamentals to Macro-Financial Integration: The Evolving Predictability of Bitcoin Returns" (SSRN, 2025–26): crypto-native variables such as funding rates and open interest predict BTC returns in every regime, while macro-financial variables gain power after 2019 (snippet only; the SSRN page returned 403). — [SSRN 6725492](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6725492)
- Vendor commentary on exchange flows and reserves (CryptoQuant Quicktakes, Glassnode Insights, news write-ups) is descriptive; none of the pages found reports a hit rate, horizon test or out-of-sample result for a netflow rule. Example: a July 2024 piece notes miner-to-exchange flows fell about 36% while BTC rose from about $58.6K to about $64K, and itself cautions that lower exchange flows do not prove accumulation. — [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/bitcoin-price-direction-miner-to-exchange-flow/)
- Coin Metrics Community API (keyless, CC BY-NC 4.0): base `https://community-api.coinmetrics.io/v4`; "API key is not required when accessing community endpoints"; documented limit 10 requests per 6 seconds per IP, up to 10 parallel requests, 429 on excess, `X-RateLimit-*` headers. — [docs.coinmetrics.io/api/v4](https://docs.coinmetrics.io/api/v4). Probed 2026-10-22: `GET /timeseries/asset-metrics?assets=btc&metrics=FlowInExNtv,FlowOutExNtv,SplyExNtv,FlowInExUSD&frequency=1d` returns data (2026-10-20: FlowInExNtv 22,038 BTC, FlowOutExNtv 21,756 BTC, SplyExNtv 2,681,408 BTC, FlowInExUSD $1.83B); BTC exchange flow/supply history starts 2011-04-24, ETH 2015-07-30; rows carry `-status: "flash"` (preliminary) and a status-time about 2–3.5 hours after UTC midnight. `FlowNetExNtv` is not a metric name ("not supported"); compute net = in − out. ETH flows are free (2026-10-20 FlowInExNtv 217,898 ETH). For xrp, doge, ada, ltc, link the community feed returned AdrActCnt and CapMVRVCur but no exchange-flow fields; `usdt_eth` SplyExNtv is "not available with supplied credentials"; sol and avax returned no supported metrics; bnb's last row was 2019-04-22. Rate-limit headers observed on the probe: `x-ratelimit-limit: 6000;w=20` with `x-ratelimit-plan: download` (differs from the documented 10/6 s; treat the documented figure as the floor). (probed)
- CryptoQuant API: Basic (free) plan is listed at 10,000 monthly credits, 10 requests/min, 30 days of history, with on-chain data not included; Professional is the first plan with on-chain data (snippet of cryptoquant.com/apis; the page returned 403 to fetch). FAQ: "API access is available on CryptoQuant pricing plans", "Rate limits are set per pricing plan", "Every call deducts credits from your monthly plan grant". Quickstart example endpoint: `/v1/btc/exchange-flows/reserve`. — [CryptoQuant API plans](https://cryptoquant.com/apis), [FAQ](https://docs.cryptoquant.com/guides/faq.md), [Quickstart](https://docs.cryptoquant.com/guides/quickstart)
- Glassnode: "A Standard (Free) account will have access to all Basic metrics at a 24 hour resolution" (web charts). "Full API access is available on the Professional plan"; "Advanced subscribers get the more limited Light API"; "To get API access, reach out to our sales team." No statement grants API access to free accounts. — [Glassnode FAQ](https://docs.glassnode.com/further-information/faq), [Timestamps and resolutions](https://docs.glassnode.com/data/timestamps-and-resolutions)
- Blockchain.com Charts API (keyless): `https://api.blockchain.info/charts/{slug}?timespan=all&format=json&sampled=false`, daily `{x,y}` values. Working slugs (probed): hash-rate (6,496 daily points from 2009), miners-revenue, n-transactions, n-unique-addresses, market-price, market-cap, estimated-transaction-volume-usd, trade-volume, difficulty, transaction-fees-usd, total-bitcoins, n-transactions-excluding-popular, utxo-count (hourly). `mvrv` and `nvt` slugs return `{"status":"not-found"}`. No exchange-flow series. The 2-year n-unique-addresses pull had six missing days. Latest point on 2026-10-22 was dated 2026-10-19/20. (probed)
- bitcoin-data.com (BGeometrics) free plan: "No token required", "last 4 years", 8 requests per hour and 15 per day; the comparison table names "Exchange flow metrics" and "Miner flow metrics" as free features but the free endpoint list contains no exchange-flow or miner-flow path. — [BGeometrics free features](https://portal.bgeometrics.com/bguser/free-features.html)
- Dune: "Free access after a trial is view-only and does not include API exports, data writes, or alerts." — [Dune billing docs](https://docs.dune.com/api-reference/overview/billing)
- CoinGlass has no free API plan; the cheapest is Hobbyist at $29/month, 30 requests/min. — [CoinGlass pricing](https://www.coinglass.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- For BTC, the two academic results disagree in sign and the newer one finds nothing economically usable; a daily net-inflow rule with R² of order 0.001 cannot clear a 0.25% round trip at a one-day horizon. The literature's consistent finding is about volatility (inflows calm or precede volatility changes), which is a position-sizing input, not a long-only entry.
- The usable free netflow feed is Coin Metrics for BTC and ETH only. Given its ~3 h post-midnight latency, the app's close-of-day D decision can only use D−1 flows, adding a day of staleness to a signal whose evidence is already at intraday speed.
- Altcoin exchange flows are not available free in any source checked; the Binance USDT-pair universe cannot get a per-coin flow signal without a paid vendor.

### Gaps
- No independent, cost-inclusive backtest of a daily exchange-netflow or exchange-reserve rule (hit rate at 1–30 days) was found; vendor posts assert bottoms and tops after the fact.
- Hoang & Baur's coefficient magnitudes and the JBF version's exact horizons were not obtained (SSRN and ScienceDirect blocked fetches).
- Bitquery and Arkham free tiers were not verified (search quota exhausted). Treat them as unconfirmed.

## 2. Stablecoin supply, stablecoin exchange reserves, SSR and large mints: do they lead prices by days to weeks?

### Takeaway
The best-identified studies find causality running from crypto prices to stablecoin issuance, not the reverse (Kristoufek 2021: "no evidence of stablecoins boosting the prices of other cryptoassets"); the positive results are either from the 2017 Tether era, intraday (USDT exchange inflows at 1–2 hours), or regime-conditional (a 2026 preprint). DefiLlama's keyless API supplies daily aggregate and per-coin supply from 2017-11-26, but stablecoin exchange reserves are not free anywhere checked, and no published test of the Stablecoin Supply Ratio was found.

### Cited Findings
- Kristoufek, "Tethered, or untethered? On the interplay between stablecoins and major cryptoassets", Finance Research Letters 43 (2021) 101991: "we find no evidence of stablecoins boosting the prices of other cryptoassets" and "the increased stablecoins issuances come in reaction to the other cryptoassets price changes", read as reflecting demand to invest in crypto. — [RePEc listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v43y2021ics1544612321000726.html)
- Griffin & Shams (2020; 2017 data, pre-ETF era): Tether flows arrived after downturns and predicted positive subsequent BTC returns; attributed "at least half of the rise in the Bitcoin price in the year from March 2017" to the practice. Contested. — [Economics Observatory](https://economicsobservatory.com/why-has-the-price-of-bitcoin-risen-fallen-in-the-past-day-week-month); [Quantpedia discussion](https://quantpedia.com/a-very-influential-paper-about-tether-bitcoin-relationship-manipulation/)
- Wei (2018, Economics Letters), the counter-study: "no empirical evidence supporting the notion that Tether grants cause subsequent Bitcoin returns to rise on a daily basis"; grants were timed with bearish moves. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/linked/644/new-study-finds-impact-of-tether-on-bitcoin-price-to-be-statistically-insignificant)
- Ante, Fiedler & Strehle (2021): 1,587 stablecoin transfers of $1M or more show higher trading volume before and after the transfer; a companion event study of 565 issuances finds market downturns in the week before issuance and positive abnormal returns in the 24 hours around issuance (snippet only; 2021 data, pre-ETF). — [Technological Forecasting and Social Change](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0040162521002833); [Finance Research Letters 41](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v41y2021ics1544612320316810.html)
- Chi, Chu & Hao (2025): USDT net inflows into exchanges "positively predicts BTC and ETH returns at multiple intervals" (1–2 hours; BTC 2-hour coefficient 6.3×10⁻⁶, t = 4.196, adjusted R² 0.002) and negatively predict ETH volatility and BTC 6-hour volatility; the daily and weekly specifications reported are for volatility only and insignificant. — [arXiv 2411.06327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06327)
- Saggu (2022, Finance Research Letters 49): BTC price responds more to Tether minting when Whale Alert tweets about it, a short-term price rise strongest in bullish sentiment (as described by Whale Alert's research page). — [Whale Alert academic research](https://whale-alert.io/academic-research.html)
- "Stablecoin Liquidity as a Crypto-Native Regime Signal: State-Dependent Density Forecasts for BTC, ETH, and SOL" (Research Square preprint rs-9715935 v1, 2026): daily BTC, ETH, SOL from April 2020 to March 2026; homogeneous and non-homogeneous hidden Markov models with three volatility states; in medium-volatility regimes stablecoin volume growth is positively associated with returns while stablecoin transaction-count growth is negatively associated; the paper reframes stablecoin activity "not as a constant linear return predictor, but as a crypto-native signal of changing market regimes"; density-forecast gains reported, coefficient evidence strongest for BTC and ETH. Not peer reviewed; v1. (snippet only; the page served only its title) — [Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-9715935/v1)
- A December 2025 VAR paper in the Academic Journal of Management and Social Sciences reports USDT and USDC peg deviations Granger-causing BTC returns with weaker reverse causality (low-tier venue; snippet only). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/399220796_The_Impact_of_Stablecoins_on_Bitcoin_Returns_An_Empirical_Analysis_Based_on_VAR_Model)
- Marketing claim to discount: a BDC Consulting write-up claims timing BTC on USDT supply spikes lifted ROI to 229%; no method, not peer reviewed. — [BDC Consulting](https://bdc.consulting/insights/cryptocurrency/a-new-study-finds-that-using-usdt)
- Stablecoin Supply Ratio: CryptoQuant defines SSR as BTC market cap divided by total stablecoin market cap and reads high values as weak buying power; a glossary repeats an unsourced "SSR below 25" rule. No statistical test of SSR was found. — [CryptoQuant user guide](https://userguide.cryptoquant.com/cryptoquant-metrics/stablecoin/stablecoin-supply-ratio); [Spark glossary](https://www.spark.money/glossary/stablecoin-market-cap)
- DefiLlama free API: "Free API — no authentication required." Stablecoin endpoints: `GET /stablecoins?includePrices=true` (all stablecoins with circulating, circulatingPrevDay/PrevWeek/PrevMonth, per-chain breakdown), `GET /stablecoincharts/all?stablecoin={id}` (historical summed market cap; array of `{date, totalCirculating, totalCirculatingUSD, totalUnreleased, totalBridgedToUSD...}`), `GET /stablecoincharts/{chain}`, `GET /stablecoin/{asset}` (per-coin history and chain distribution), `GET /stablecoinchains`, `GET /stablecoinprices`. The docs list them under `api.llama.fi`; the same paths are served at `stablecoins.llama.fi` (probed). No rate limit is stated. — [DefiLlama API docs (free)](https://api-docs.defillama.com/llms-free.txt). Probed 2026-10-22: `/stablecoincharts/all` begins at unix 1511913600 (2017-11-26) with daily rows; USDT (`id` 1) circulating 182.48B with `circulatingPrevDay` 183.02B and `circulatingPrevWeek` 184.00B; full `/stablecoincharts/all` payload is about 470 KB, so cache it and pull daily deltas from `/stablecoins`. (probed)
- bitcoin-data.com `GET /v1/stablecoin-supply` (free, keyless): 1,456 daily rows from 2022-10-21 to 2026-10-20 with fields usdt, usdc, dai, busd, gusd, pax (2026-10-20: usdt 183.48B, usdc 72.06B). Subject to the 8/hour, 15/day limit. (probed) — [BGeometrics free features](https://portal.bgeometrics.com/bguser/free-features.html)
- Stablecoin exchange reserves: CryptoQuant's exchange-reserve endpoints are paid (on-chain excluded from Basic); Coin Metrics community refuses `SplyExNtv` for `usdt_eth` ("not available with supplied credentials"). (probed; see question 1 sources)

### Inferences
- Aggregate stablecoin supply moves slowly (USDT changed −0.3% day over day and −0.8% week over week on 2026-10-21), so as a daily entry trigger it is nearly constant; its only defensible use is as a slow regime variable (e.g. 30-day supply growth sign), and even that rests on a regime-conditional preprint plus 2017-era evidence.
- The one stablecoin signal with a positive sign in the post-2020 sample (USDT exchange inflows) is intraday and needs exchange-flow data the app cannot get free for USDT.
- Peg-deviation signals could be computed from Binance USDC/USDT or FDUSD/USDT tickers keylessly, but the only supporting study is low-tier.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed test of SSR, SSR-RSI or "large mint" rules at 1–30 day horizons was found.
- Kaiko, Glassnode, Galaxy and Coin Metrics State of the Network stablecoin research could not be searched (quota exhausted); none appears in the findings above.
- Research Square preprint details (which stablecoin metric, data source, out-of-sample window) come from a snippet; the page itself served no abstract.

## 3. Valuation and cycle metrics (MVRV, MVRV-Z, SOPR/aSOPR, NUPL, realized price, Puell, hash ribbons, 200-week MA, Pi Cycle) as regime filters: out-of-sample record and free sources

### Takeaway
A 2026 real-time audit finds every classic top-calling threshold (Pi Cycle, MVRV > 3.7, Mayer > 2.4, Puell > 4.0, 5× 2-year MA) was precise through 2017, fired 210–240 days early for the November 2021 top and was silent at the October 2025 top, while the bottom thresholds (MVRV < 1.0, Puell < 0.5, Mayer < 0.8) marked the 2015, 2018 and 2022 lows within weeks; stock-to-flow and Metcalfe's law have "limited to no ability to predict Bitcoin's returns out-of-sample". For free data, bitcoin-data.com serves MVRV, MVRV-Z, SOPR/aSOPR, NUPL, realized price, Puell and hash ribbons keylessly over 4 years but embargoes the valuation metrics 7 days; Coin Metrics community serves current MVRV (CapMVRVCur) for BTC and several large coins.

### Cited Findings
- "Bitcoin Runs on a Clock: Why Every Price Indicator Dies and the Halving Clock Doesn't" (arXiv 2607.26188, 2026): five top-callers checked against mechanically dated tops (ATH followed by a drawdown of at least 45%), HIT = within 60 days. Pi Cycle: hits in Apr 2013 (−3 d), Dec 2013 (+1 d), Dec 2017 (0 d), Apr 2021 (−1 d); 210 days early for Nov 2021; no fire in Oct 2025. MVRV > 3.7: hits −21, −26, −10, −31 days; 240 days early for Nov 2021; no fire 2025. Mayer > 2.4: hits +25, +19, −19, −31 days; −240 d; no fire 2025. Puell > 4.0 and price > 5× 2-year MA: hits in 2013 and 2017 only, silent since. Bottom callers: "MVRV < 1.0, Mayer < 0.8 and Puell < 0.5 all marking the November 2022 bottom within a week"; MVRV < 1.0 and Puell < 0.5 marked the 2015, 2018 and 2022 bottoms within four weeks; minimum MVRV this cycle 1.15 and price has not touched the 200-week MA. The 200-week MA "failed in both directions: the December 2018 bottom formed just above it, and 2022 broke well below it." Mechanism: per-epoch oscillator maxima shrink every cycle (MVRV 5.88 → 4.72 → 3.96 → 2.74), so "recalibrating thresholds only bets that compression has stopped." Caveats from the paper itself: the turns were dated ex post, so indicator "precision" is retrospective; the author discloses running a media channel promoting the halving framework. — [arXiv 2607.26188](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26188v1)
- Shelton, "Bitcoin Return Prediction: Is It Possible via Stock-to-Flow, Metcalfe's Law, Technical Analysis, or Market Sentiment?", JRFM 17(10):443 (2024): "the stock-to-flow model predictions and Metcalfe's Law help to explain Bitcoin's returns in-sample" "but have limited to no ability to predict Bitcoin's returns out-of-sample"; sentiment and technical measures "are poor predictors of Bitcoin's returns out-of-sample". The author then reports an "OOS-blended model trading strategy" switching BTC/cash that boosts CAPM alpha "by almost 1300 basis points" with higher Sharpe and Sortino and much lower drawdown. — [RePEc listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jjrfmx/v17y2024i10p443-d1490211.html); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/17/10/443)
- Stock-to-flow as the cautionary case: Morningstar finds the original model's slope constant "has failed to predict bitcoin's subsequent returns". — [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/alternative-investments/what-is-bitcoins-scarcity-worth)
- Pi Cycle Top: an independent test notes "Only 3 trades isn't a big enough sample" and that the fit "raises the question whether some serious data overfitting has happened." — [Medium backtest](https://medium.com/@d-kozh/btc-pi-cycle-top-indicator-test-d55a09b80daf)
- Overfitting method: a hold-out sample "does not control for the number of trials involved in a discovery"; with enough configurations a backtest can be fit to any target; the deflated Sharpe ratio adjusts for trials, skewness and kurtosis. — [Bailey et al.](https://sdm.lbl.gov/oapapers/ssrn-id2507040-bailey.pdf)
- Fieberg, Günther, Poddig & Zaremba, "Non-standard errors in the cryptocurrency world", IRFA 92 (2024) 103106: 20,736 research designs across 43 sorting variables; size and momentum are robust, the other sorting variables are not (as summarized by the survey below). — [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)
- bitcoin-data.com (BGeometrics) free endpoints, base `https://bitcoin-data.com/v1/`, "No token required", "last 4 years", 8 requests/hour and 15/day, each with `/last`, `/csv`, `?day=`, `?startday=&endday=`: `/mvrv`, `/mvrv-zscore`, `/sth-mvrv`, `/lth-mvrv`, `/sopr`, `/sth-sopr`, `/lth-sopr`, `/asopr`, `/nupl`, `/nupl-sth`, `/nupl-lth`, `/realized-price`, `/sth-realized-price`, `/lth-realized-price`, `/realized-cap`, `/puell-multiple`, `/hashrate`, `/hashprice`, `/hashribbons`, `/nvts`, `/cdd`, `/vdd-multiple`, `/hodl-waves-supply`, `/thermo-price`, `/aviv`, `/fear-greed`, `/funding-rate`, `/open-interest-futures`, `/etf-flow-btc`, `/stablecoin-supply`, `/bitcoin-dominance`, address-cohort balances. — [BGeometrics free features](https://portal.bgeometrics.com/bguser/free-features.html). Probed 2026-10-22: `/v1/mvrv/last` → `{"d":"2026-10-14","mvrv":1.5249,"delayed":true,"message":"Real-time data (last 7 days) requires an active subscription"}`; `/v1/sopr/last` → 0.9987 and `/v1/nupl/last` → 0.3442, both dated 2026-10-14 and delayed; `/v1/hashribbons/last` is current (2026-10-20, sma_30 1.0013×10⁹, sma_60 9.712×10⁸, "Up") and `/v1/hashribbons` returns 1,461 daily rows from 2022-10-21. (probed)
- Coin Metrics community `CapMVRVCur` is free and current for btc (2026-10-20: 1.5376), eth (1.0791), xrp (0.897), doge (0.699), ada (0.611), ltc (0.799), link (1.008); `CapRealUSD`, `SOPR`, `SplyAct1yr`, `NVTAdj`, `IssContNtv`, `RevUSD` are "not available with supplied credentials". BTC history begins 2009-01-03 (MVRV null in the earliest rows). (probed) — [docs.coinmetrics.io](https://docs.coinmetrics.io/api/v4)
- Glassnode's MVRV endpoints (`/v1/metrics/market/mvrv`, `mvrv_z_score`, `price_realized_usd`) list 10m/1h/24h resolutions but require a paid API plan (see question 1). — [Glassnode market endpoints](https://docs.glassnode.com/basic-api/endpoints/market)
- LookIntoBitcoin: charts "free to use" and updated daily; data access is via a licensed API for businesses and a Pro plan at $1,188/year; no free data download confirmed. — [LookIntoBitcoin hash ribbons](https://www.lookintobitcoin.com/charts/hash-ribbons/)
- Blockchain.com has no MVRV or NVT chart slug; NVT can be built from `market-cap` divided by `estimated-transaction-volume-usd`, both free and daily. (probed)

### Inferences
- The asymmetry in the 2026 audit (tops unreliable, bottoms reliable) argues for using MVRV, Puell and Mayer only as "cheap region" filters for a long-only app, never as exit triggers; but with three or four prior bottoms in the sample, this is a handful of observations, and the paper's own ex-post dating inflates precision.
- The 7-day free embargo on bitcoin-data.com valuation metrics makes them unusable for daily timing but adequate for a regime flag that changes over weeks; MVRV via Coin Metrics is same-day (D−1 at the close) and covers several large coins, so a per-coin MVRV < 1 filter is feasible free for BTC, ETH, XRP, DOGE, ADA, LTC, LINK but not for most of the Binance top-50.
- Shelton's blended timing result should be read as a warning rather than a recipe: it was constructed after the univariate out-of-sample failures were observed, which is the researcher-degrees-of-freedom problem Bailey describes.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed out-of-sample test of SOPR/aSOPR or NUPL rules at daily to monthly horizons was found; the 2026 audit does not mention NUPL.
- The cost of acting on bottom thresholds (how long capital sits idle, how often MVRV < 1 is reached) was not quantified in any source.

## 4. Premium indices from public tickers: Coinbase premium and Korea (kimchi) premium

### Takeaway
No academic test finds the Coinbase premium predicts next-day or next-week BTC returns; vendor posts treat it as sentiment commentary. The kimchi premium literature (2024 Economic Modelling, 2026 Mathematics) shows threshold mean reversion of the premium itself and time-varying lead-lag with gold and KOSPI, not a documented BTC-return edge; the one claim of a zero-crossing edge (+1.7% at 7 days, +6.2% at 30 days in 2025) is journalistic and unverified. Both premiums are computable keylessly from Coinbase, Upbit and Frankfurter endpoints with history to 2016–2017.

### Cited Findings
- Seo, Koo & Yang, "Nonlinear dynamics of Kimchi premium", Economic Modelling 135 (2024): threshold regression; the premium "is mean-reverting when it exceeds a certain level of thresholds but displays a random walk inside the range"; long-run steady state 1.24% for Bitcoin; trading fees correlate positively with the threshold. ## 5. US spot BTC and ETH ETF daily flows (since January 2024): do yesterday's flows predict returns?

### Takeaway
The most careful study (386 sessions, 11 Jan 2024–28 Jul 2025) finds the naive same-day coefficient of 4.89 pp per $1B shrinks to 1.67 pp once the return window starts after the ETF session, and "a window that starts after the flow is public shows no positive association", while prior BTC returns robustly predict same-day flows; a 2024 FalconX VAR found a small positive flows-to-price term (0.027) with a 3–4 day impulse peak of about 1.2%. Flows are free as an HTML table at Farside (from 11 Jan 2024, US$m, negatives in parentheses, rows still filling the next morning) and as a daily BTC-denominated series at bitcoin-data.com.

### Cited Findings
- Fabus, Vartiak, Stalmasekova & Kicova, "Spot Bitcoin ETF Flows and Bitcoin Returns: Timestamp Alignment and Asymmetric Feedback" (SSRN 7545019, posted 30 Sep 2026): daily spot BTC ETF flows and BTC prices at 16:00 New York, 386 sessions from 11 January 2024 to 28 July 2025. Under naive same-day UTC pairing the estimate is 4.89 percentage points per USD 1 billion; aligning the return window to the 24 hours after the session close reduces it to 1.67 (HAC p = 0.007, adjusted R² = 0.036); "a window that starts after the flow is public shows no positive association." A prior 24-hour BTC return ending before the ETF day predicts same-day flows under every timing convention; in a VAR, lagged returns predict flows while lagged flows predict returns only marginally; Engle-Granger rejects no cointegration, Johansen is not robust; the flow-to-return coefficient does not differ between cash-creation and in-kind regimes. (abstract via search snippet and ML-Quant; SSRN page returned 403) — [SSRN 7545019](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7545019); [ML-Quant summary](https://www.ml-quant.com/papers/ssrn/7545019/)
- Lim, "The Price Impact of Spot Bitcoin ETF Flows" (SSRN 6592830, April 2026): same-day impact of about 53 basis points of BTC return per $100 million of net flow; bidirectional Granger causality with flows predicting next-day returns and returns predicting later flows (snippet only). — [SSRN 6592830](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6592830)
- FalconX, "What can spot ETF flows tell us about the trajectory of Bitcoin prices?" ### Inferences
- For a daily-close app, a $500M inflow day implies roughly +0.8% expected BTC move under the 1.67 pp aligned coefficient, above the 0.25% fee, but that window begins at the 16:00 ET ETF close, before flows are published; the window that starts after publication shows no association, and the next UTC open (00:00 UTC, 20:00 ET) is after publication. ## 6. Whale and smart-money tracking (Arkham, Nansen, Lookonchain, Hyperliquid whales, Whale Alert): is copying or fading profitable after costs?

### Takeaway
Independent tests say no: Presto Labs' event study of 2021–2024 whale deposits to Binance (BTC, ETH, SOL) yields R² of 0.0017–0.0537 and concludes deposits "lack sufficient predictive power to be a reliable trade signal"; a Hyperliquid copy-trading replay with fees and slippage lost money on 14 of 19 leaders; the academic literature finds whale alerts move volatility and herd retail for about 24 hours, not direction. Nansen's own documentation says following smart money is not automatically profitable.

### Cited Findings
- Chung & Ha (Presto Labs, 2025), "Whale Alerts: are they tradable?": large deposits into Binance for BTC, ETH and SOL, 1 January 2021 to 27 December 2024; Whale Alert thresholds of $50M/$50M/$20M scaled to Binance's ~40% spot share, giving $20M/$20M/$8M; deposit counts from Dune, entity labels from Arkham plus the authors' own work; outcome measured as maximum drawdown in 1-hour and 6-hour windows; "R-squared ranged from 0.0017 to 0.0537"; 879 ETH whale deposits of which 538 (61%) were VCs and market makers; conclusion: "large exchange deposits are weak predictors for price declines" and "whale exchange deposits lack sufficient predictive power to be a reliable trade signal." No 1-day horizon, hit rate or cost analysis reported. — [Presto Labs](https://www.prestolabs.io/research/whale-alerts-are-they-tradable)
- Hazen, Jagtiani & Mester, "How Do Large, Sophisticated Cryptocurrency Trades Impact Broader DeFi Market Dynamics?" (Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia WP 26-42, September 2026): transaction-level BTC, ETH and WBTC data matched to Whale Alert signals; whale alerts "heavily reshape (native) BTC participation by mobilizing previously inactive retail and institutional investors"; "non-whale investors exhibit strong leader-follower behavior in the BTC market -- buying and selling in tandem with whale direction"; "Whale alerts induce a brief 24-hour spike in BTC volatility" but compressed volatility on Ethereum. No claim of return predictability. — [Philadelphia Fed WP 26-42](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/working-papers/2026/wp26-42.pdf)
- Magner & Sanhueza, "The Moby Dick effect: Contagious Bitcoin whales in the crypto market", Finance Research Letters 85 (2025): Whale Alert data April 2022 to May 2025; whale transfers transmit shocks to the top-15 coins' returns mainly at 6 and 24 hours; the immediate effect is small and false positives (internal and collateral moves) are a large share of alerts (per Whale Alert's summary and a secondary snippet). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S154461232501164X); [Whale Alert academic research](https://whale-alert.io/academic-research.html)
- Herremans & Low (arXiv 2211.08281): Whale Alert tweets plus CryptoQuant data improve forecasts of next-day BTC volatility spikes with a Synthesizer Transformer; volatility, not direction. — [arXiv 2211.08281](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.08281)
- Hyperliquid copy-trading replay (hl-copy-core README, data to 2026-10-13): 34 candidate leaders, 155k fills, "19 leaders had a fill in the 30-day lookback (73,054 changes)"; follower equity $10,000, 5 bp slippage, 4.5 bp fee; 4 of 19 leaders ended positive, 1 flat, 14 negative; "sum over the 19: -5,621 USD at 10k, -56,383 at 100k"; with risk caps (max leverage 3, $5,000 per symbol, $500 daily loss) "the sum over the 19 is -2,495 USD and 5 end positive"; rank-3 example: 60 USD of fees on 134k turnover, follower 5.28% versus leader 7.66%; "median skip rate is 91%"; funding, liquidations, partial fills and latency not modelled. — [hl-copy-core on GitHub](https://github.com/winterchill-trading/hl-copy-core)
- Hyperliquid whale simulation (Medium): 43 addresses over 77 days; following only accounts of $50M or more gave a 98.6% win rate and +12% PnL, copying all whales about 61.5%; costs not clearly included; tiny sample (snippet only; page returned 403). — [Medium](https://medium.com/@gwrx2005/combining-simulation-and-machine-learning-analysis-of-whale-trading-on-hyperliquid-93f10d96941b)
- Nansen's own material defines Smart Money by past realized PnL, win rate and trade history and warns that following it "is not automatically profitable" because such wallets can be early, wrong or hedging; its copy-trading tutorial on one wallet with a 66% hit rate concluded "it's still too early to tell if it's copy trade worthy." Independent reviews note the label is evidence of activity, not of the next trade's profit, and that it "can encourage late copy-trading." No backtest of copying labelled wallets after fees was found. — [Nansen Hyperliquid guide](https://nansen.ai/post/how-to-trade-hyperliquid-perps-with-smart-money-data-(2026-guide)); [Nansen copy-trading template](https://docs.nansen.ai/guides/templates/complex-use-cases/use-case-4-copytrading-top-performing-wallets); [Nansen 2022 tutorial](https://www.nansen.ai/post/the-compass-sept-21-2022)
- Whale Alert's academic page is vendor-curated; it lists ten papers, of which only Saggu (2022) and Magner & Sanhueza (2025) concern returns, and both at hours-long horizons. — [Whale Alert academic research](https://whale-alert.io/academic-research.html)

### Inferences
- Every positive result in this area is intraday (6–24 hours) and about volatility or contagion; nothing supports a next-day long-only entry on Binance spot after a whale deposit or a smart-money buy. Fading large exchange deposits (the natural short) is both the wrong side for the app and, per Presto, not predictive.
- Copy-trading leaderboards are selected on past profit, so any apparent edge carries survivorship bias; the only cost-inclusive replay found was negative in aggregate.

### Gaps
- No independent study of Arkham or Lookonchain-based signals, and no cost-inclusive test of Nansen labels, was found; Arkham API access terms were not verified.
- Presto's per-asset figures are in image charts and were not extracted.

## 7. Miner flows and miner capitulation (hash ribbons, miner reserves, miner-to-exchange transfers) as regime signals; evidence since 2022

### Takeaway
Hash ribbons rest on about 9–14 signals since 2013 (Capriole: 9 signals, average gain to the next cycle peak "over 5000%", average max drawdown 11%, with a hindsight disclaimer; an independent count: 14 signals, 64.29% profitable, average trade 253 days, no exit rule or fees stated). The live 2025–26 episode was a counterexample: capitulation began in late November 2025 near $81–90K, lasted about three months, and BTC fell to about $60K before recovering. Hash rate is free and current from several keyless sources; miner reserves and miner-to-exchange flows are paid-only (CryptoQuant; Coin Metrics miner metrics are refused on the community feed), and miners' debt-driven and AI-pivot selling since 2024 confounds the signal's premise.

### Cited Findings
- Capriole (Charles Edwards), "Hash Ribbons & Bitcoin Bottoms" (2019, editorial note 2023): buy when the 1-month SMA of hash rate crosses above the 2-month SMA ("the worst of the miner capitulation is typically over"), with a price filter requiring the 10-day price SMA above the 20-day; "9 historic buy signals"; hash-rate-only entries show an average gain to the next cycle peak of "over 5000%" with average maximum drawdown 11%; January 2015 was a "bad" signal with a 42% drawdown; capitulation happens "on average just once a year"; results are hypothetical and "generally prepared with the benefit of hindsight." — [Capriole](https://capriole.com/hash-ribbons-bitcoin-bottoms)
- Independent tally: "Since 2013, there have been 14 blue dots (or Bitcoin buy signals)", 64.29% profitable, average trade 253 days, entries at the first signal after each capitulation; no exit rule, average return, drawdown, fees or data source stated; "only back-tested results". — [Stopsaving.com](https://www.stopsaving.com/bitcoin-hash-ribbons-indicator/)
- CoinDesk, 27 Nov 2025: hash ribbon (30-day vs 60-day hash-rate averages, Glassnode chart) entered capitulation as BTC fell to about $81,000 on Nov 21 and rebounded to about $90,000; named prior signals May 2021, June 2022, November 2022; the metric "can also appear before downtrends that did not mark major bottoms, citing May and July 2025"; hash rate about 15% below its high, hashprice at a five-year low, miners diversifying into AI/HPC. — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2025/11/27/hash-ribbon-flashes-signal-that-often-marks-cyclical-bottoms-for-bitcoin-price)

- Free hash-rate sources (probed 2026-10-22): Blockchain.com `GET https://api.blockchain.info/charts/hash-rate?timespan=all&format=json` (6,496 daily values from 2009, TH/s, latest 2026-10-19); mempool.space `GET https://mempool.space/api/v1/mining/hashrate/all` (keyless, `hashrates[{timestamp, avgHashrate}]` from 2009; also `/3y`, `/1y`); Coin Metrics community `HashRate` (btc 2026-10-20: 1.153×10⁹ TH/s); bitcoin-data.com `/v1/hashribbons` (1,461 daily rows from 2022-10-21 with sma_30, sma_60 and an Up/Down flag, current to 2026-10-20, not embargoed), `/v1/hashrate`, `/v1/hashprice`, `/v1/puell-multiple`, `/v1/difficulty-BTC`. — [BGeometrics free features](https://portal.bgeometrics.com/bguser/free-features.html)

(probed; see question 1)

### Inferences
- The signal is rare (roughly yearly), so fees are irrelevant, but 9–14 observations cannot distinguish a 64% hit rate from chance, and the 2025–26 episode shows a ~33% further drawdown after the capitulation flag before any recovery cross; the recovery cross (30-day back above 60-day) would have come around March 2026 near $60–65K, and BTC traded near $82–84K on 2026-10-21 (Coinbase ticker, Blockchain.com market-price), which would count as a win for that one instance but is a single observation.

- For the app, hash ribbons are at most a BTC-only regime permission (allow longs after the recovery cross), computable from any of the free hash-rate feeds; Puell is computable from Blockchain.com miners-revenue divided by its 365-day mean.

### Gaps
- No cost-inclusive, out-of-sample study of hash ribbons, miner reserves or miner-to-exchange flows since 2022 was found; evidence is vendor charts and news.
- The exact dates of the 2026 recovery cross and the full 2025–26 outcome were not obtained from a primary series (the 8/hour limit on bitcoin-data.com stopped a full pull of the hashribbons history for inspection).

## 8. Network-activity factors from the academic literature (active addresses, transaction counts, NVT) and whether a long-only, liquid-coin version survives costs

### Takeaway
Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu show new-address innovations explain more than 8% of weekly return variation but "do not forecast cryptocurrency returns in the future", while the price-to-new-address ratio sorts 127 coins (Feb 2018–Jun 2021, Glassnode, weekly) into a long leg earning 1.5% per week gross with 11.4% weekly volatility and a long-short spread of 1.9% per week; the effect halved post-2020 and a 20,736-design replication finds only size and momentum robust among 43 crypto sorting variables. Free active-address and transaction data exist for BTC, ETH and a few large coins (Coin Metrics, Blockchain.com), not for the Binance top-50, so a cross-sectional network factor is not buildable free.

### Cited Findings
- Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu, "Accounting for Cryptocurrency Value" (October 2021 version): Glassnode on-chain data; measures built at weekly frequency; sample "from February 2018 ... to June 2021", stablecoins excluded, "The final sample consists of 127 cryptocurrencies"; average logged coin return 0.3% per week with weekly standard deviation 0.251; new-address innovations "can account for more than eight percent of the variations of cryptocurrency returns"; "innovations to the number of new addresses do not forecast cryptocurrency returns in the future" (no post-drift) and returns do not precede releases (no pre-drift). Price-to-new-address ratio pa = log(Price/NA), weekly quartile sorts of about 30 coins each: lowest-pa quartile 1.5% per week excess return, highest −0.4%; spread 1.9% per week, significant at 1%; weekly standard deviations 11.4% (low quartile), 10.5% (high), 6.8% (long-short); weekly Sharpe 0.132 (0.951 annualised) for the long leg and 0.279 (2.012 annualised) long-short; alpha versus the crypto 3-factor model significant at 1%; long-short "remains significant at the 10 percent level 20 weeks after the portfolio formation period"; robustness excluding Bitcoin (Table A.6). No transaction-cost or turnover analysis appears in the text. — [Yale working paper PDF](https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-10/onchain_v18.pdf)
- Survey, "Cryptocurrency as an Investable Asset Class: Coming of Age" (arXiv 2510.14435v4, 2025–26): the value proxy CVALUE (price-to-new-address) long-short Q5−Q1 mean −0.035 (t = −3.50) full sample and −0.023 (t = −2.32) post-2020 (Newey-West); adoption explains about 8% of return variation and more than 10% for the largest coins; the survey excludes coins under $1 million market cap and warns that portfolio magnitudes are "illustrative upper bounds" that "depend on strong assumptions about... investability (liquidity, custody, and regulatory constraints)"; it cites Fieberg et al. (2024): 20,736 research designs for 43 sorting variables, with size and momentum robust and the other sorting variables not. — [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)
- Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu, "Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency" (NBER w25882; Journal of Finance 2022): market, size and momentum factors capture the cross-section; equity factor models do not. — [NBER working paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25882/w25882.pdf)
- Cong, Karolyi, Tang & Zhao (EFMA 2022): 4,007 crypto assets; "value" and "network adoption" premia; five-factor C-5 model (snippet only). — [EFMA paper](https://www.efmaefm.org/0efmameetings/efma%20annual%20meetings/2022-rome/papers/efma%202022_stage-3032_question-full%20paper_id-231.pdf)
- "Blockchain characteristics and cryptocurrency returns" (Journal of International Financial Markets, Institutions and Money, 2023): a two-factor model of the value-weighted market and a "network distribution factor" explains the cross-section (snippet only). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1042443123000562)
- Shelton (2024): Metcalfe's-law (active-address) variables explain BTC returns in-sample but have "limited to no ability to predict Bitcoin's returns out-of-sample" (time series, single asset). — [RePEc listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jjrfmx/v17y2024i10p443-d1490211.html)
- Older: Yang et al. (2015) use transaction-network complexity measures and report improved BTC return and volatility prediction (pre-2016 data). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/304289854_Bitcoin_Market_Return_and_Volatility_Forecasting_Using_Transaction_Network_Flow_Properties)
- Free network-activity data (probed 2026-10-22): Coin Metrics community `AdrActCnt` and `TxCnt` daily for btc (648,048 active addresses, 800,236 transactions on 2026-10-20), eth (782,317), xrp (98,845), doge (33,075), ada (13,450), ltc (261,595), link (3,476); bnb stale since 2019-04-22; sol and avax unavailable; `TxTfrValAdjUSD`, `NVTAdj`, `VelCur1yr` refused. Blockchain.com `n-unique-addresses` (6,468 daily points from 2009), `n-transactions`, `n-transactions-excluding-popular`, `estimated-transaction-volume-usd`, `market-cap` (BTC only). bitcoin-data.com `/v1/nvts` (BTC, free, 4 years). — [docs.coinmetrics.io](https://docs.coinmetrics.io/api/v4); [BGeometrics free features](https://portal.bgeometrics.com/bguser/free-features.html)

### Inferences
- A long-only version of the value factor means holding the cheapest quartile of about 30 coins, rebalanced weekly: 1.5% per week gross on 2018–2021 data, roughly halved after 2020 per the survey, against a 0.40% round trip on each weekly rebalance and 11.4% weekly volatility; with unknown turnover the net figure cannot be stated, and most quartile members in a 127-coin universe are below Binance top-50 liquidity.
- The paper's own test says new-address growth does not predict returns (no drift), so "rising active addresses → buy" is contradicted by the best evidence; only the valuation ratio (price relative to new addresses) has any predictive record, and only cross-sectionally.
- Free data cover at most seven large coins' active addresses, which is too few for quartile sorts; a BTC-only NVT or active-address time-series rule has no supporting out-of-sample evidence (Shelton).

### Gaps
- Fieberg et al.'s own paper was not read; its finding is taken from the survey.
- Cong et al. and the JIFMIM two-factor paper were not read; their portfolio returns, costs and liquidity screens are unknown.
- No study was found that restricts network factors to top-50 liquid coins and reports net-of-fee results.
