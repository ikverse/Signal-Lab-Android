# Systematic quantitative evidence on crypto return predictability, 2023-2026 (beyond plain momentum and breakouts)

Research date: 2026-10-22. Fee frame used throughout: 0.25% round trip BTC/ETH, 0.40% other top-50, 0.75% below rank 50. "Gross" means before costs; "L/S" means long-short. Most academic tables are weekly, value-weighted, L/S and gross; where the long leg alone is reported it is called out. Sources that could not be opened (paywalled ScienceDirect/Springer/SSRN pages) are marked "abstract/snippet only".

A note on attribution: the brief names the crypto trend factor as "Liu, Zhang and Zhou, JFQA 2024". The published crypto paper is Fieberg, Liedtke, Poddig, Walker and Zaremba, "A Trend Factor for the Cross Section of Cryptocurrency Returns", JFQA 60(7), Nov 2025, pp. 3116-3153 (accepted 2024). It builds on the equity trend-factor work of Han, Zhou and Zhu (2016) and Liu, Zhou and Zhu (2024). Numbers below are from the open-access JFQA text.

## 1. The crypto factor zoo after 2022: which long-only legs in liquid coins survive 0.4% round trip?

### Takeaway
The only post-2022-published factor with a reported long leg, a liquid-coin subset, turnover and break-even costs is the multi-indicator trend composite CTREND (JFQA 2025): its top quintile among the 100 largest coins earned 3.78%/week gross and 3.11-3.38%/week net at 30-50 bps per trade with 67% weekly turnover (sample Apr 2015-May 2022, so nothing post-ETF). Every other replication-grade study (Fieberg-Liedtke-Zaremba 2024, Cakici et al. 2024, Mercik-Zaremba-Demir 2026) says size, volume, lottery and most liquidity premia live in micro-caps, momentum's alpha is mostly in the short leg, and anomalies fade over time and concentrate in bull markets.

### Cited Findings
- CTREND is built by machine-learning aggregation of 28 technical signals (moving averages, MACD, Bollinger, CCI, RSI, volume and volatility indicators) across horizons on >3,000 coins, weekly rebalanced, value-weighted quintiles, Apr 2015-May 2022. Full-sample Table 3: Low quintile 0.12%/week (t=0.16), High quintile 3.98%/week (t=4.80, annualized Sharpe 1.80), H-L 3.87%/week (t=5.19, Sharpe 1.94). The High quintile's CCAPM alpha is 2.11%/week (t=3.49) but its alpha against the LTW market-size-momentum model is only 1.10%/week (t=1.94, not significant at 5%); the H-L LTW alpha is 2.62% (t=4.22). Top features: boll_mid, cci, macd. — [Fieberg et al., JFQA 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/trend-factor-for-the-cross-section-of-cryptocurrency-returns/4C1509ACBA33D5DCAF0AC24379148178)
- Liquid/large subsets (Table 8): among the 100 largest coins the High quintile earns 3.79%/week (t=4.44) and H-L 3.39% (t=4.49); among the 10% largest H-L is 2.51% (t=3.01); among the 10% most liquid (Amihud) H-L is 2.20% (t=2.76). Authors: "the CTREND effect originates mainly from the biggest and most liquid cryptocurrencies." — [Fieberg et al., JFQA 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/trend-factor-for-the-cross-section-of-cryptocurrency-returns/4C1509ACBA33D5DCAF0AC24379148178)
- Costs (Table 9): assumed 30 bps long / 40 bps short per trade (I), 40/50 (II), 50/60 (III). All coins: long-leg (H) turnover 62.84%/week, gross 3.98%, net 3.61% / 3.48% / 3.36%, break-even cost (BETC) 3.17% per trade, BETC at 5% significance 1.86%. Top-100 coins: H turnover 66.74%, gross 3.78%, net 3.38% / 3.24% / 3.11%, BETC 2.83%, BETC-5% 1.56%; H-L top-100 net 2.45% to 1.90%, BETC 1.25%, BETC-5% 0.70%. — [Fieberg et al., JFQA 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/trend-factor-for-the-cross-section-of-cryptocurrency-returns/4C1509ACBA33D5DCAF0AC24379148178)
- Decay and regime (Table 5): first half (Apr 2015-Nov 2018) H quintile 3.87%/week, H-L 5.41%; second half (Nov 2018-May 2022) H quintile 1.56%/week (t=1.93, not significant), H-L 2.55% (t=3.01) with the L quintile at -0.72%. Bear-market weeks: H quintile 1.12% (t=1.49, not significant), H-L 2.44% (t=2.47). Low-volatility weeks H-L 4.94% vs high-volatility weeks 3.02%. — [Fieberg et al., JFQA 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/trend-factor-for-the-cross-section-of-cryptocurrency-returns/4C1509ACBA33D5DCAF0AC24379148178)
- Holding period: moving from weekly to 2-week rebalancing cuts the average weekly return by 1.5 pp to 2.34%; returns stay significant at 5% up to 4-week holds; "most cryptocurrency momentum strategies no longer produce significant profits at a 3- or even 2-week horizon." — [Fieberg et al., JFQA 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/trend-factor-for-the-cross-section-of-cryptocurrency-returns/4C1509ACBA33D5DCAF0AC24379148178)
- Fieberg, Liedtke and Zaremba, "Cryptocurrency anomalies and economic constraints", IRFA 94 (2024): size and volume anomalies come from micro-caps "with negligible economic importance"; momentum persists in larger coins "but it carries substantial trading costs, and its alphas come mostly from short positions"; "Most abnormal returns occur primarily in bull markets and fade over time"; recommends protocols that focus on long positions, costs, hard-to-trade coins and recent years (abstract only). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v94y2024ics1057521924001509.html)
- Fieberg, Liedtke, Metko and Zaremba (Quantitative Finance 23(12), 2023) replicated 34 anomalies on >3,900 coins 2014-2022; past winners consistently outperform losers (snippet only). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v23y2023i12p1853-1869.html)
- Cakici, Shahzad, Będowska-Sójka and Zaremba, "Machine learning and the cross-section of cryptocurrency returns", IRFA 94 (2024): benefits of model complexity are "limited"; predictability comes from a few simple characteristics (market price, past alpha, illiquidity, momentum); unlike stocks, "abnormal returns come from the long leg of the trade and persist over time"; most ML strategies stay profitable after costs despite high turnover, but alphas "are concentrated in hard-to-trade assets and depend on extreme returns in small, illiquid, volatile coins" (abstract only). A 2026 review summarizes the same paper as OLS beating regression trees and neural nets out of sample. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v94y2024ics1057521924001765.html); [Baquero 2026 review](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00071v1)
- Mercik, Zaremba and Demir, "Crypto factor zoo (.Zip)", IRFA 113 (2026): applies the Swade et al. iterative alpha-based factor selection to 36 crypto factors; "just two to three factors can eliminate all significant portfolio alphas"; the selected factors are turnover volatility, bid-ask spreads and the new-address-to-price ratio, i.e. liquidity dominates (abstract only; sample dates not visible). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v113y2026ics1057521926000645.html)
- Cong, Karolyi, Tang and Zhao, "Value Premium, Network Adoption, and Factor Pricing of Crypto Assets" (working paper, 2021-22, 4,007 coins, fundamentals for 616): momentum appears only in the largest-cap group with reversals in other size groups; value (price-to-network/NVT-type) and network-adoption premia exist and the value premium is larger for small coins; preferred model MKT+SMB+VAL+NET+MOM. Pre-2022 sample, gross, L/S. — [ABFER paper PDF](https://abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2022/annual-conference/papers-investfin/AC22P3048_Value-Premium-Network-Adoption-and-Factor-Pricing-of-Crypto-Assets.pdf)
- Bianchi and Babiak, "A Factor Model for Cryptocurrency Returns" (CERGE-EI WP 710, 2021; later "A risk-based explanation of cryptocurrency returns"): IPCA with three latent factors and time-varying loadings beats observable-factor models; momentum sorts produce positive unconditional returns. No 2023-26 momentum/trend paper by these authors was found. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cer/papers/wp710.html)
- Lottery/MAX: a Financial Innovation (2021) paper finds MAX positively predicts future crypto returns ("MAX momentum"), while another study finds a 1-SD increase in MAX corresponds to a 0.043% decline in subsequent returns; an Emerald four-factor model reports a crypto illiquidity factor earning 2.30%/week and a crypto MAX factor (CLMH) 1.77%/week, both gross L/S. Sign disagreement; no 2024-25 paper surfaced. — [Financial Innovation 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40854-021-00291-9); [Emerald four-factor](https://www.emerald.com/cafr/article/27/4/493/1271913/Unravelling-cross-sectional-patterns-in); [negative-sign study (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348412182_Speculation_and_lottery-like_demand_in_cryptocurrency_markets)
- Idiosyncratic volatility: Zhang and Li (RIBAF 2020) find IVOL positively related to expected crypto returns (opposite sign to equities); a Finance Research Letters 2026 paper, "Revisiting the low-volatility anomaly in cryptocurrency markets", reports a significantly negative volatility risk premium in Fama-MacBeth regressions post-2017 (abstract only). — [Zhang & Li 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0275531920301926); [FRL 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612326003818)
- Volatility of liquidity (FRL 2026): high-minus-low "volatility of illiquidity" quintile portfolios earn weekly three-factor alphas up to 1.70% over 2014-2024 (abstract only; gross L/S). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612326013875)
- Crypto-volatility-beta: "The price of crypto-market volatility risk" (RIBAF 2026) finds a significant negative premium for assets with high beta to Bitcoin implied volatility (abstract only). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0275531926001017)

### Inferences
- For a long-only top-50/100 universe, the credible candidate from the factor zoo is a cross-sectional composite of several trend indicators (CTREND-style ranking), not size, volume, MAX, skewness or IVOL. At the app's 0.40% round trip (0.20% per side), the top-100 long leg's break-even of 1.56-2.83% per trade leaves a wide margin on paper, but the second-half (2018-22) long-leg return of 1.56%/week was not statistically significant on its own and has beta about 1.0 to the crypto market, so much of it is market exposure plus the bull-market component the IRFA 2024 paper warns about.
- Nothing in the published factor literature covers 2023-2026 or the post-ETF regime; all decay evidence points the same way (first-half vs second-half CTREND, "fade over time" in Fieberg-Liedtke-Zaremba 2024). Treat all weekly-return magnitudes as upper bounds.
- The Mercik-Zaremba-Demir result (liquidity variables absorb all alphas) implies that anything the app ranks on should be checked against a liquidity sort; if a signal's long leg is just "illiquid coins", it will not survive a top-50 filter.
- Value proxies (NVT, price-to-realized) have only pre-2022, gross, L/S evidence that is stronger in small coins; no post-2022 test was found.

### Gaps
- No study reports the long leg alone for a top-50 Binance-listed universe with 2023-2026 data; CTREND stops at May 2022.
- CTREND's exact 28 indicators, horizons and the ML aggregation step are in the paper's Section III and supplementary material (not extracted here); replicating it in the app needs that list.
- Downside beta and skewness: the Journal of Banking & Finance 2021 "Downside risk and the cross-section of cryptocurrency returns" paper was located by title only; no 2023-26 update found.
- No survivorship-bias-corrected post-2022 factor dataset or OpenAssetPricing-style public crypto factor library was found.

## 2. Low volatility and betting-against-beta in crypto

### Takeaway
Three Finance Research Letters papers disagree by sample: no low-vol premium on 1,000 coins 2013-2019, but a "statistically and economically meaningful" low-vol premium after 2017, strongest with 2-3 month volatility formation and 1-month holds, and in liquid coins the long low-vol leg is where the return is while the short high-vol leg adds nothing. No study tests whether a long-only low-vol tilt beats simply holding BTC, and none reports net-of-cost numbers in the abstracts available.

### Cited Findings
- "Cryptocurrencies and the low volatility anomaly" (FRL, 2020/21): 1,000 cryptocurrencies 2013-2019, "no evidence of a significant low volatility premium" (snippet only). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S154461232030667X)
- "Low-volatility strategies for highly liquid cryptocurrencies" (FRL 2021/22): "long portfolios which invest in low volatility CCs generate significantly positive returns, whereas short portfolios which invest in high volatility CCs do not" (snippet only; page returned 403). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1544612321004116)
- "Revisiting the low-volatility anomaly in cryptocurrency markets" (FRL 2026): a meaningful low-volatility premium in the post-2017 period; the earlier high-volatility premium "reversed as the market matured"; strongest spread from 2-3 month volatility formation and 1-month holding; Fama-MacBeth shows a significantly negative volatility risk premium; the volatility effect survives controls for size, value and momentum using standard deviation and beta but is not significant after controlling for the beta effect (abstract/snippet only; ResearchGate summary appears mixed with another paper, treat with care). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612326003818); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/402991237_Revisiting_the_low-volatility_anomaly_in_cryptocurrency_markets)
- Related: the crypto-volatility-beta paper (RIBAF 2026) finds high-vol-beta coins carry a negative premium (abstract only). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0275531926001017)
- Habeli, Barakchian and Motavasseli, "Adaptive Risk Allocation in Crypto Markets: Evaluating Volatility-Scaled Portfolios" (SSRN 5090097) exists but the page returned 403; title only. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5090097)

### Inferences
- The evidence pattern (long leg works, short leg does not; effect appears as the market matures) is favourable for a long-only app, and a 60-90 day realized-vol sort with monthly rebalancing is cheap at 0.40% round trip because turnover is low.
- "Low-vol beats high-vol" is not "low-vol beats BTC": in most samples BTC is itself in the low-vol bucket of the cross-section, so a low-vol tilt within the top 50 may largely reproduce a BTC/ETH overweight.
- The result that the volatility effect loses significance after controlling for beta suggests the tradable version is a low-beta (to BTC) tilt rather than low total volatility.

### Gaps
- No paper found with a long-only low-vol portfolio benchmarked against BTC buy-and-hold, nor any net-of-cost figure.
- Sample periods, universes and exact spreads of the 2026 FRL paper are behind a paywall.
- No betting-against-beta (leverage-neutral) construction tested on crypto was found for 2023-2026.

## 3. Signal combination: ensembles, agreement filters, averaging

### Takeaway
Two independent 2025-26 crypto backtests agree that volatility scaling, not signal diversification, produces most of the Sharpe and drawdown improvement; adding a trend signal on top of vol-scaling added about 0.05 Sharpe in one test, and a plain 200-day BTC long/flat rule matched a diversified three-horizon ensemble. Peer-reviewed crypto evidence that requiring agreement of uncorrelated signals raises net Sharpe is thin, high-frequency and pre-2022; a 2025-26 BTC/ETH study found momentum-plus-reversal rule gains not significant after correcting for 300 variants tried.

### Cited Findings
- Summitward/Engineer Investor backtest (BTC, ETH, SOL daily Coinbase closes, Jan 2017-Jun 2025; equal-weight 1-, 3- and 12-month TSMOM, long/short, 60-day vol scaling, 10% vol target; costs 1-4 bps per trade; one sample, no OOS split): trend strategy CAGR 6.3%, vol 8.1%, Sharpe 0.79, max DD -14.6%; BTC buy-and-hold Sharpe 1.13, DD -83.8%; vol-managed equal-weight with no trend signal Sharpe 1.24, DD -19.9%; 200-day long/flat BTC-only Sharpe 0.89, DD -10.4%. Decomposition: raw trend without vol scaling Sharpe 0.71 (DD -80%), full trend 0.79 (DD -15%); "the trend signal added about 0.05 Sharpe". Delaying trades 5 days cut Sharpe 0.79 to 0.60; bootstrap CI on Sharpe roughly 0.1 to 1.5; turnover 7-8x per year. The two Sharpe tables in the article are internally inconsistent (B&H 1.13 vs 0.73). — [Summitward](https://summitward.com/learn/crypto-trend-following)
- Bui and Nguyen, arXiv 2602.11708 (150+ Binance perpetuals, 6-hour bars, calibration 2021, out-of-sample Jan 2022-Dec 2024, 4 bps taker fee plus volume-based slippage plus funding): Vol-Scaled TSMOM (10% target) Sharpe 1.83, ann. return 22.8%, MDD -16.1%; unscaled TSMOM-1M Sharpe 0.65, MDD -34.8%; TSMOM-3M 0.54; their long-short AdaptiveTrend 70/30 Sharpe 2.41, MDD -12.7%; BTC buy-and-hold Sharpe 0.17, MDD -64.1%; equal-weight top-20 B&H Sharpe 0.07. Ledoit-Wolf block bootstrap: Vol-Scaled TSMOM vs AdaptiveTrend p=0.024. Reported Sharpe for AdaptiveTrend does not match its stated return/vol (about 2.14 implied); whether the TSMOM benchmarks are long-only is not stated. — [arXiv 2602.11708](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1)
- Marrazzo, "Trend-Following: Mostly a Volatility Story" (review, no own backtest), citing Zakamulin (2026, 1995-2025 multi-asset): long-only trend Sharpe 0.74 vs buy-and-hold 0.61 vs long-short 0.55 vs model-implied vol-timing 0.76; Moskowitz et al. (2025): nonlinear TSMOM Sharpe 0.83 vs linear 0.70 at 12-month lookback and 0.47 vs 0.33 at 3-month, with a binary in/out rule worse than both; Sepp and Lucic (2025): SG Trend net Sharpe 0.38. Conclusion: "The edge is risk timing, not return prediction." — [Substack](https://antoniomarrazzo.substack.com/p/trend-following-mostly-a-volatility)
- JRFM 19(9):692 (2025/26), lagged momentum and reversal rules on BTC and ETH across daytime/overnight sessions: realized Sharpe differences "are not statistically significant in paired bootstrap tests, and Hansen's Superior Predictive Ability test does not reject the null after accounting for the search over 300 cutoff-strategy combinations." — [JRFM](https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm19090692)
- "High frequency momentum trading with cryptocurrencies" (RIBAF 2020): an ensemble requiring five models to agree ("Ensemble 5") gave the best results for ETH and LTC, annualized Sharpe 0.80 and 0.91 after 0.5% round-trip costs; high-frequency and pre-2020. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0275531919308062)
- Practitioner blog (Medium, not peer-reviewed): a 50/50 blend of a momentum and a mean-reversion strategy reported Sharpe 1.71, between the components (about 1.0 and 2.3). — [Medium](https://medium.com/@briplotnik/systematic-crypto-trading-strategies-momentum-mean-reversion-volatility-filtering-8d7da06d60ed)
- Quantpedia, "Revisiting trend-following and mean-reversion strategies in Bitcoin" (daily Gemini opens from Nov 2015; in-sample to Aug 2024; out-of-sample Feb 2022-Aug 2024; no costs): MAX rule (buy when price makes an x-day high, x=10..50) stays effective OOS with 20-day better than 10-day; MIN rule (buy x-day lows) "performed poorly, with low or negative returns" OOS; the MIN+MAX combination is weakened by its MIN leg; no day-of-week effect found. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/revisiting-trend-following-and-mean-reversion-strategies-in-bitcoin/)
- "Mitigating false signals in crypto-asset trading using a binary confirmation mechanism" (Chaos, Solitons & Fractals, 2026) exists but returned 403; title only. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960077926007344)

### Inferences
- For the app, the highest-value "combination" step is not adding more entry rules but sizing every existing call by inverse realized volatility (or a portfolio vol target); both crypto tests and the multi-asset literature point the same way, and it is cheap in fees because sizing changes can be batched with existing rebalances.
- Evidence for AND-gating (require Donchian and SMA(50) and 2-week momentum to agree) is anecdotal in crypto; the SPA-test result on BTC/ETH shows how quickly 300 variants erase apparent gains. Any agreement filter should be judged against the app's random-entry baseline with the variant count recorded.
- Nonlinear shaping of the trend signal (scale position by signal strength, with a cap, rather than binary in/out) has multi-asset support (Moskowitz et al. 2025) and is compatible with long-only.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed crypto study of signal-agreement filters on daily bars 2023-2026 was found.
- Baltas-Kosowski-style multi-signal crypto work was not located.

## 4. Volatility targeting and regime filters: which improve net results rather than only drawdowns?

### Takeaway
Volatility scaling has the strongest and most consistent evidence: in the arXiv 2602.11708 out-of-sample test it took 1-month TSMOM from Sharpe 0.65 to 1.83 and raised annual return (18.4% to 22.8%) while cutting MDD from -35% to -16%; in the Summitward test it cut drawdown from -80% to -15% but left Sharpe nearly unchanged (0.71 to 0.79) and lowered CAGR. The 200-day BTC gate reduces drawdown far more than it adds return. No tested evidence exists for BTC dominance, ETH/BTC, altcoin-season or vol-of-vol filters; the Nasdaq link is strong but asymmetric and untested as a rule; among macro predictors only the dollar index showed trading value in one 2025 study. The Fear and Greed index does not predict returns.

### Cited Findings
- Vol scaling (see Q3 for details): Vol-Scaled TSMOM Sharpe 1.83 / return 22.8% / MDD -16.1% vs TSMOM-1M 0.65 / 18.4% / -34.8%, OOS 2022-2024, net of 4 bps + slippage + funding. — [arXiv 2602.11708](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1); Summitward: raw trend 0.71 (DD -80%) vs vol-scaled trend 0.79 (DD -15%); vol-managed B&H 0.74 vs B&H 0.73 in the decomposition table. — [Summitward](https://summitward.com/learn/crypto-trend-following)
- 200-day BTC long/flat: Sharpe 0.89, max DD -10.4%, CAGR 6.2% vs BTC B&H Sharpe 1.13, DD -83.8%, CAGR 73.5% (Jan 2017-Jun 2025, 1-4 bps per trade). — [Summitward](https://summitward.com/learn/crypto-trend-following)
- Karassavidis, "Quantitative Evaluation of Volatility-Adaptive Trend-Following Models in Cryptocurrency Markets" (SSRN 5821842, Nov 2025): BTC and ETH, moving-average trend + RSI filter + ATR position sizing, parameters optimized by Latin Hypercube Sampling with 5,000,000 samples across 5 configurations; secondary summaries report Sharpe 1.54 (BTC) and 1.80 (ETH) net of Binance futures fees over 2020-2025, CAGR 20%/24%, MDD near 11%. A commentator flags the five-year single-uptrend sample and the optimization intensity. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5821842); [Marrazzo commentary](https://antoniomarrazzo.substack.com/p/trend-following-mostly-a-volatility)
- Concretum, "Catching Crypto Trends": rotational portfolio of the top-20 most liquid coins, ensemble of Donchian-channel models with different lookbacks, volatility-based sizing; Sharpe "above 1.5, net of fees", annualized alpha 10.8% vs Bitcoin; period, costs and long/short status not stated on the page. — [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/catching-crypto-trends-a-tactical-approach-for-bitcoin-and-altcoins/)
- Le and Ruthbah, "Trend-following Strategies for Crypto Investors" (SSRN 4551518): "the effect of transaction costs is very substantial on portfolio performances" (snippet; page returned 403). — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4551518)
- Nasdaq/risk-on: LSEG data via Quartz put the 2025 average BTC-Nasdaq 100 correlation at 0.52 vs 0.23 in 2024; K33 reports 30-day correlation above 0.7 again in 2026 but "periods where 30-day Nasdaq returns exceeded 10% have typically coincided with declining BTC beta"; Bitget reports about 0.80 in late 2025/early 2026 then a breakdown as BTC fell from its Oct 2025 peak; arXiv 2501.09911 describes the correlation as regime-dependent, higher in accommodative/risk-on states; several sources describe downside asymmetry (BTC falls harder when equities fall). No timing rule is tested in any of them. — [FXStreet/K33](https://www.fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/bitcoin-fails-to-match-us-equities-surge-despite-strong-nasdaq-correlation-k33-202605122306); [arXiv 2501.09911](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09911); [Wintermute](https://www.wintermute.com/insights/views/opinions/btc-and-the-nasdaq-the-correlation-that-only-hurts); [Bitget](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605273235)
- Arain and Snudden, "When Are Statistical Forecast Gains Economically Relevant? Evidence From Bitcoin Returns", Journal of Forecasting (Dec 2025): real-time OOS daily bivariate VAR forecasts, Oct 2021-Feb 2024, 12 predictors; "most predictors fail to generate trading profits even when they are statistically significant"; only the USD index and the Shanghai composite produced significant excess profits, "and only during periods of large price swings"; MSFE is uninformative about profitability. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/for.70077); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5868483)
- Fear and Greed: a 2026 ScienceDirect study (daily 2018-2025, VAR/Granger) finds index changes do not Granger-cause returns and give no out-of-sample gain; returns lead sentiment. A Springer 2026 study (2,945 days, 2018-2026) finds Extreme Fear persists next day 82.0% of the time; a 1-SD rise is linked to higher forward returns but the 7-day CI includes zero; expanding-window forecasts do not beat the historical mean. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S305070062600006X); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42521-026-00218-y)
- Altcoin Season Index definition: share of the top-100 altcoins (ex-stablecoins) beating BTC over the prior 90 days; Bitcoin season at 25 or below, altcoin season at 75 or above. Readings around 20 Oct 2026: 57 with BTC dominance 60.1%; yearly high 64-67 (late Sep 2026), yearly low 14-15 (mid Dec 2025); providers disagree on exact values. No backtest found. — [CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/charts/altcoin-season-index/); [TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/investing/35642); [Cryptorank](https://cryptorank.io/charts/altcoin-index)
- BTC dominance caveat: raw BTC.D is distorted by stablecoin growth ("understates Bitcoin's actual share by 4-6 points in 2026" per one vendor's proprietary adjustment). — [Lambda Finance](https://www.lambdafin.com/articles/bitcoin-dominance-history)

### Inferences
- Vol targeting is the one regime technique with two independent crypto tests, one with a bootstrap and a true OOS window, both net of realistic fees. Its net-return effect is sample-dependent (positive in 2022-24 perps, slightly negative in 2017-25 spot); its drawdown effect is unambiguous.
- The 200-day gate behaves as the literature predicts: it is a drawdown tool. Using it to *size* rather than to *block* calls is consistent with the nonlinear-TSMOM finding that binary in/out is the worst shaping.
- BTC dominance, ETH/BTC trend and the altcoin-season index are lore: widely quoted, never tested in anything found. If the app adds one, it must be treated as a new registered strategy with its own random baseline.
- A dollar-index filter has one peer-reviewed data point (Arain-Snudden) and is consistent with the risk-on narrative, but 2 of 12 predictors surviving is roughly what multiple testing predicts.

### Gaps
- No study of realized-vol terciles or vol-of-vol as a conditioning variable for crypto trend returns.
- No published backtest of BTC dominance, ETH/BTC ratio, or the altcoin-season index as a filter.
- The Nasdaq-correlation sources are media/industry notes with conflicting numbers; no peer-reviewed timing test post-ETF.

## 5. Seasonality post-2022

### Takeaway
Post-2022 academic work finds calendar effects mostly in volatility and higher moments rather than mean returns, with day-of-week results that shift across samples (Thursday/November in one 2024 eleven-coin study; Monday and reverse-January for BTC in older work); turn-of-month is absent outside BTC. FOMC and CPI days produce robust abnormal volatility (about 0.8-1.0 pp of daily vol, p<0.001) but no significant directional return in a 2020-2025 event study. "Uptober" rests on 10 of 13 positive Octobers with no formal test, and the gains often reverse by December. No study of Deribit expiry Fridays was found.

### Cited Findings
- Algieri, Lawuobahsumo, Leccadito and Zahid, "Calendar effects on returns, volatility and higher moments: Evidence from crypto markets", NAJEF 79 (2025): BTC, DASH, DOGE, LTC, XRP, XLM (published version adds ETH and XMR); autoregressive conditional density model with dummies for day-of-week, month, quarter, US holidays and weekends; anomalies found "in the mean, variance, skewness, and kurtosis"; no trading-rule or cost test visible (abstract only). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecofin/v79y2025ics1062940825000816.html)
- Day-of-week and month-of-year study (Turkish journal, 2024, 11 coins): strongest effects on Thursdays and in November; "the calendar effect appears dynamic rather than static" (snippet only; PDF is in Turkish). — [DergiPark](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/3521393)
- A systematic review of 15 articles: increasing evidence of a day-of-the-week effect but "the exact patterns and underlying drivers remain underexplored". — [ScienceDirect review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612321003597)
- Turn-of-month: one study finds no significant turn-of-month effect across the coins considered; another finds it in Bitcoin but not other coins (older samples, snippets). — [Revisiting seasonality (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379995324_Revisiting_seasonality_in_cryptocurrencies); [Seasonality in Cryptocurrencies, FRL](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612318304513)
- Quantpedia's Bitcoin seasonality re-test (hourly Gemini, Oct 2015-Jun 2023): the 21:00-23:00 UTC long window gives 40.6% annualized with -22.7% MDD in-sample, no costs, no significance tests, and "a rough period in 2022 and 2023"; day-of-week ranking of that window puts Friday first; no monthly test. (Hour-of-day is already dropped by the app; included only because it shows the 2022-23 decay.) — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/the-seasonality-of-bitcoin/)
- Uptober: BTC closed October higher in 10 of 13 years 2013-2025, mean +19.1%, median +13.8%; a 2016-2025 sample gives mean 17.8%, median 12.7%, 80% win rate; October 2025 closed about -3.7% to -3.9%; measured 30 Sep to 31 Dec only 5 of 9 years were positive and the median fell from +10.9% to +5.4%. No source provides a significance test. — [CoinJuice](https://coinjuice.com/research-hub/bitcoin-october-seasonality-13-years-of-data); [CryptoTicker](https://cryptoticker.io/en/uptober-q4-returns-recalculated/); [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bitcoin-s-seasonality-and-2026-outlook-can-the-pattern-break)
- FOMC/CPI event study (Nazaruk, KSE master's thesis, 2025; 1 Jan 2020-31 May 2025; 62 CPI and 42 FOMC events after overlap removal; Parkinson and Rogers-Satchell range volatility; abnormal volatility in pp of daily vol): CPI day abnormal vol 0.81 pp (p<0.001, about 15 pp annualized), builds from t-2 (0.70 pp) and fades after; FOMC day 1.03 pp (p<0.001), with t-2 at 1.26 pp (p=0.022) and t+1 at 0.60 pp (p=0.034); cumulative abnormal returns insignificant on every day and window; by sub-group the only return effect is the pre-window CAR of -3.36% before rate hikes (n=11, p=0.029). — [KSE thesis PDF](https://kse.ua/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/illia-nazaruk_268722_assignsubmission_file_nazaruk_final_thesis.pdf)
- Journal of Mathematical Finance (2024), high-frequency data: crypto market volatility significantly increases on FOMC announcement days. — [SCIRP](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=136168)
- Ma et al. (2022, 2018-2021 Fed surprises, as summarized in the thesis): tightening surprises push BTC lower by about 0.25% on the meeting day (not verified against the original). — [KSE thesis PDF](https://kse.ua/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/illia-nazaruk_268722_assignsubmission_file_nazaruk_final_thesis.pdf)
- ETF flows and macro days: of the 20 largest absolute spot-ETF flow days, 7 fall within three sessions of an FOMC or CPI date, versus about 10 expected by chance because 49% of sessions are within three sessions of such a date. — [CryptoETF.today](https://cryptoetf.today/en/blog/macro-impact-on-bitcoin-etf-flows)
- Deribit: the Dec 2024 expiry was the largest in Deribit's history (44% of total open interest); no study of returns around monthly expiries was found. — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2024/12/24/record-14-b-bitcoin-options-expiry-looms-as-market-looks-highly-levered-up)

### Inferences
- At a daily hold with 0.25-0.40% round trip, a seasonal mean effect must exceed roughly 0.3-0.4%/day to break even; none of the documented day-of-week or turn-of-month means is reported at that size for 2023-2026, and the two most recent academic studies locate the effects in variance and skewness.
- FOMC/CPI days are a volatility event, not a direction event: the evidence supports reducing position size or widening stops around them (the app already sizes by ATR), not entering on them.
- Uptober/December are n=13 month-of-year observations; the 7-of-9 vs 5-of-9 reversal when the window is extended to year-end is the clearest sign of a story fitted to the data.

### Gaps
- No post-2022 study reports day-of-week or turn-of-month mean returns net of costs, nor the number of calendar effects tested (a multiple-testing denominator).
- Deribit monthly expiry Fridays: no evidence found either way.
- First-days-of-quarter effect: no crypto-specific study found.

## 6. Statistical arbitrage: ETH/BTC mean reversion, cointegration pairs, basket-vs-BTC

### Takeaway
The one 2024 peer-reviewed crypto pairs study with net results (Tadi and Witzany, Financial Innovation 2024) earned a net Sharpe of about 0.9-1.0 (37% annualized) with hourly Binance USDT-margined futures over Jan 2021-Jan 2023 at a 0.04% taker fee, during a period when BTC lost 17%/year; the same approach at 5-minute frequency produced "drastically negative" net returns, and every variant needs a short leg in futures. No published 2023-2026 test of ETH/BTC-ratio mean reversion or basket-vs-BTC spreads was found.

### Cited Findings
- Tadi and Witzany, "Copula-based trading of cointegrated cryptocurrency pairs" (arXiv 2305.06961; Financial Innovation 2024): 20 coins, hourly closes, Binance USDT-margined futures, Jan 2021-Jan 2023, 104 rolling cycles of 3 weeks formation + 1 week trading; BTCUSDT is the reference asset; pairs selected by Engle-Granger (EG) or KSS cointegration tests then ranked by Kendall's tau; the two highest-tau altcoins are traded by going long one spread and short the other (BTC itself is not traded). Net of 0.04% taker fees: EG with entry threshold 0.10 gives total return 76.2%, annualized 37.1%, ann. vol 38.2%, Sharpe 0.97, MDD -35.6%, 176 transactions, costs 11.7% of gross P&L; KSS 0.10 gives 35.3%/yr, Sharpe 0.93; wider thresholds degrade (KSS 0.20: 10.4%/yr, Sharpe 0.16, costs 47.7% of gross P&L). Benchmarks over the same window: BTC buy-and-hold -17.0%/yr, Sharpe -0.23, MDD -77.1%; 20-coin equal-weight 14.4%/yr, Sharpe 0.15. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.06961v2); [Financial Innovation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40854-024-00702-7)
- Search summary of the published version: 5-minute data "produced much higher returns than hourly data, but it also faced astronomical transaction costs, leading to drastically negative net returns"; hourly results were "also heavily impacted by transaction costs". — [Financial Innovation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40854-024-00702-7)
- "Pairs Trading in Cryptocurrency Markets" (IEEE Access 2020): gross hourly cointegration returns about 4% per month; "exogenous factors such as transaction costs or execution windows are extremely important" (older). — [IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/9200323)
- "Optimal market-neutral currency trading on the cryptocurrency platform" (arXiv 2405.15461) exists; not opened. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15461)

### Inferences
- Pairs/cointegration is a futures strategy for the app: both legs are needed, the edge is largest at hourly frequency where spot fees (0.40% round trip vs 0.08% futures) would consume roughly the whole gross P&L given that futures fees already took 12-48% of it.
- The 2021-23 sample is a bear market in which market-neutral strategies look best relative to buy-and-hold; there is no 2024-2026 out-of-sample evidence.
- A long-only approximation (buy the lagging coin of a cointegrated pair, hold the other in cash) has no published test and would carry full market beta.

### Gaps
- ETH/BTC ratio mean reversion: no quantitative study 2023-2026 found.
- Basket-vs-BTC spread trading: none found.
- No pairs study using 2024-2026 data.

## 7. Triangular and cross-exchange arbitrage for a retail Binance spot user at 100-500 ms

### Takeaway
A Finance Research Letters 2025 study of one week (3-9 June 2024) of Binance order-book data found 4,879 triangular opportunities on BTC/USDT-LTC/BTC-LTC/USDT, 96.9% of which were unprofitable even at the lowest VIP-9 fee tier; a regular-fee trader had 18 profitable opportunities worth $12-18 for the week on an average tradable size of $4,071, and would need to execute within 146 ms on average to avoid an expected loss. At 100-500 ms from a phone this is not viable. Cross-exchange spot arbitrage has no peer-reviewed 2024-25 profitability study; practitioner sources agree spreads on majors are removed within milliseconds and withdrawal fees/delays dominate.

### Cited Findings
- Muck, Schmidl and Wolf, "Wish or reality? On the exploitability of triangular arbitrage in cryptocurrency markets", Finance Research Letters 73 (2025) 106508: high-frequency bid/ask and quantity data from Binance websocket, 3-9 June 2024, BTC/USDT, LTC/BTC, LTC/USDT; latency 80 ms from a European server, reduced to 4 ms from Tokyo; 4,879 potential opportunities, 4,337 with gross return between 0% and 0.025%, three above 0.5%; about 65% last one second or less, 767 (15.7%) longer than 5 seconds; after Binance fees "96.93 percent of the triangular arbitrage opportunities are not even profitable for VIP 9 traders" (0.054% per leg); for VIP 1-2 traders (0.225% three-leg cost) 18 opportunities (0.37%) remain; taking them immediately sums to 2.01% of return for the week, best case 2.97%; average tradable amount $4,070.86, so "marginal arbitrage profits between 12.43 U.S. Dollar and 17.73 U.S. Dollar for the entire week"; a regular trader "has to execute trades on average within a maximum of 146 milliseconds" for positive expected value; conclusion: "cryptocurrency markets exhibit a high degree of efficiency." — [University of Bamberg repository PDF](https://fis.uni-bamberg.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/8b9ae900-017a-4bed-94b9-609c16e89945/content); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S154461232401537X)
- Hobbyist Binance triangular bot: 156 opportunities over 69 hours, one executed. — [shufflingbytes](https://shufflingbytes.com/posts/binance-triangular-arbitrage/)
- Practitioner guide: with 0.1% taker on three legs the gap must exceed 0.3%; automated execution "requires latency to Binance's matching engine under 10 milliseconds." — [Arbitrage Scanner](https://arbitragescanner.io/blog/binance-arbitrage-guide-fees-api-strategies)
- Cross-exchange: a 2025 preprint says profitability "is influenced by trading fees, withdrawal limits, and settlement delays" with no hard profit data; 2026 guides say simple retail spread arbitrage "is rarely profitable after fees, slippage, latency, and transfer delays", spreads on majors are removed "within milliseconds", realistic margins on majors are 0.1-0.3% per trade after fees, BTC withdrawals cost $5-30, and remaining opportunities are niche (new listings, regional fiat, illiquid alts, derivatives basis). — [ResearchGate preprint](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395786823_Cross-Exchange_Arbitrage_in_the_Digital_Asset_Market); [Startupik](https://startupik.com/cross-exchange-arbitrage-in-2026-why-most-spreads-are-dead/); [Hyrotrader](https://www.hyrotrader.com/blog/crypto-arbitrage-trading/)

### Inferences
- With the app's phone latency (100-500 ms) and standard fees, the expected value of triangular arbitrage on Binance is negative by the paper's own threshold (146 ms at 4 ms detection latency, and fee tier below VIP 1 would be worse than the 0.225% modelled).
- Cross-exchange spot arbitrage additionally requires capital on two venues and on-chain transfer time, which a once-a-day Android app cannot provide; the only "retail-feasible" variants (listing arbitrage, regional fiat premia) are not systematic signals.

### Gaps
- The Muck et al. sample is one week and one triangle; no study covers BNB/FDUSD triangles or a longer 2024-2026 window.
- No peer-reviewed measurement of cross-exchange spot spreads net of withdrawal fees for 2024-2026.

## 8. Grid and DCA bots: published or audited performance, regimes, fee drag

### Takeaway
The only formal analysis (Chen, Chen and Jang, arXiv Jun 2025) proves a static grid has zero expected profit before fees under random-walk assumptions and therefore negative expected profit after fees; its dynamic variant beat buy-and-hold on BTC (1-minute Binance data, Jan 2021-Jul 2024, 0.08% fee) "largely due to the significant rise in cryptocurrency prices", while on ETH it did not beat buy-and-hold on return and only cut drawdown from about 80% to about 50%. No audited Binance/Pionex/3Commas bot performance data was found. DCA vs lump sum on BTC: lump sum won about 66% of windows in a 2017-2023 backtest, with DCA better in post-2021 windows.

### Cited Findings
- Chen, Chen and Jang, "Dynamic Grid Trading Strategy: From Zero Expectation to Market Outperformance" (arXiv 2506.11921, Jun 2025): BTC and ETH spot, Binance 1-minute klines Jan 2021-Jul 2024, at most one transaction per minute, fee 0.08% per trade (OKX level-1 maker); Claim 2 proves "the expected value of a grid strategy is zero under our assumptions" (no fees, random price path, finite grid); with fees the traditional grid's IRR, plotted for a grid whose bounds contain the whole path (BTC 10,000-80,000; ETH 500-5,000), is flat before fees and lower after; DGT (grid re-centred dynamically) reaches IRR 60-70% in the sample, "largely due to the significant rise in cryptocurrency prices in recent years, which is highly favorable for spot grid trading"; ETH IRR higher than BTC because of higher volatility; "a small grid size results in lower profits due to the high proportion of transaction fees within the arbitrage profit"; for BTC, DGT IRR exceeds buy-and-hold with much lower MDD; for ETH, DGT IRR "is not substantially higher than that of the buy-and-hold strategy" but MDD is about 50% vs about 80%. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11921)
- Stevens FSC (Seshu and Yerramilli, 2024): Bybit level-2 order book Oct 2023-Mar 2024 and Binance second-level OHLCV Jan 2017-Apr 2024; Random-Forest and LSTM-tuned grid parameters "did not beat traditional grid methods"; a mean-reversion-based "hedging" grid variant "provided better returns than simply holding Bitcoin"; no fee assumptions, returns or drawdowns stated on the page. — [Stevens FSC](https://fsc.stevens.edu/cryptocurrency-market-making-improving-grid-trading-strategies-in-bitcoin/)
- "Grid Bot Wars" dataset (GitHub, 50 Bybit grid bots, three 31-day seasons, ATR-band grid spacing, Bybit VIP-0 fees 0.02% maker / 0.055% taker): reports a top ROI of +914.8% but no median, win rate, fee totals, drawdowns or methodology; not peer-reviewed. — [GitHub](https://github.com/antigravity-projects-hq/grid-bot-trading-dataset)
- DCA vs lump sum (BTC): a six-year 2017-2023 backtest found lump-sum beat DCA in roughly 66% of windows and accumulated 3-75% more coin, the gap widening as DCA intervals lengthen; Amdax finds lump sum has larger drawdowns and that risk-adjusted results alternate; Nakamoto Portfolio's 2021-onward chart shows DCA relatively better in that period. All are industry write-ups, not peer-reviewed. — [CoinageReport citing Yellow.com](https://coinagereport.com/what-is-dollarcost-averaging-a-steady-approach-to-buying-crypto/); [Amdax](https://medium.com/amdax-asset-management/lump-sum-or-dollar-cost-averaging-42c8f5bb9938); [Nakamoto Portfolio](https://nakamotoportfolio.com/static/docs/DCA_Lumpsum.pdf)

### Inferences
- A grid is a short-volatility, long-mean-reversion position: it harvests the fee-adjusted spread between grid levels and loses when price trends out of the range. With the app's spot fees (0.25-0.40% round trip, 3-5x the 0.08% assumed in the paper), the "small grid size" fee-drag problem the authors describe is magnified and the viable grid spacing becomes wide enough that few levels trade.
- The DGT outperformance is a bull-sample result (2021-Jul 2024 BTC roughly 3x); on ETH, which also rose, it did not add return. Nothing supports grid bots as a return source after fees; the honest claim is drawdown reduction in choppy-up markets.
- DCA is a risk-reduction schedule, not a signal; the evidence (lump sum wins about two-thirds of windows) is the same as in equities.

### Gaps
- No audited or published Binance, Pionex or 3Commas grid/DCA bot performance series were found; vendor claims are marketing.
- No study of grid bots over the 2025-2026 period or at spot-fee levels of 0.1% per side.

## 9. Machine learning on OHLCV and technical features, 2024-2026: out-of-sample net results and the replication record

### Takeaway
The record is mostly negative once costs and naive baselines are applied: a 2026 systematic review of 23 Bitcoin-prediction studies finds directional accuracy typically in the low-to-mid 50s (peak about 57%), naive models beating complex ones at 1-, 7- and 30-day horizons, OLS beating trees and neural nets cross-sectionally, and "no evidence of robust, real predictive edge over the naive baseline at one-to-six-month horizons"; the positive cross-sectional results (Cakici et al. 2024) are driven by small illiquid coins, and the main source of daily predictability found by XGBoost on 3,703 coins is one-day reversal.

### Cited Findings
- Baquero, "Bitcoin Price Prediction: Peer-Reviewed Evidence and Social Media Discourse" (arXiv 2606.00071, 2026): about 120 results screened, 40 full texts, 23 studies discussed; look-ahead via full-sample normalization flagged as a common failure; McNally et al. (2018) LSTM 52% directional accuracy on a single split; Arain and Snudden (2025) OOS accuracy "mostly in the low-to-mid 50s, peaking around 57%" and only 2 of 12 predictors yield significant excess profits, only in large swings; Puoti et al. (2024) naive models outperform complex ones at 1-, 7- and 30-day horizons; Cakici et al. (2024) OLS beats trees and neural nets OOS; Berger and Koubová (2024) a simple RNN matches ARMA-GARCH and LSTM adds nothing; Wei et al. (2023) XGBoost best but only against linear models; Gurgul et al. (2025) sentiment-augmented daily models gain vs buy-and-hold, not vs a naive forecast; Guo et al. (2024) report a cross-crypto long-short portfolio with "sizable returns after transaction costs"; social-media claims of 95-99% accuracy are in-sample MAPE on price levels. Verdict: the field's "primary need is not more models but better evaluation." — [arXiv 2606.00071](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00071v1)
- Walk-forward BTC/USDT hourly study (arXiv 2606.00060, 2026): about 70,000 observations, 27-fold walk-forward; a cost-aware execution filter that trades only when forecast magnitude exceeds a cost threshold "sharply reduces turnover and restores profitability in selected configurations"; bootstrap evidence "does not support formal statistical dominance of XGBoost". — [arXiv 2606.00060](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00060v1)
- Multi-timeframe BTC classifier (MDPI Forecasting 8(3):40, 2025 holdout): +35.97% gross on 2025 OOS data; authors state realistic round-trip fees "would likely make net returns negligible." — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-9394/8/3/40)
- "Forecasting cryptocurrency returns with machine learning" (RIBAF 2023): 3,703 coins 2013-2021; expanded XGBoost OOS R-squared 4.855%, attributed largely to a one-day reversal effect. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0275531923000314)
- Filippou, Rapach and Thimsen (SSRN 3914414, working paper): 41 cryptocurrencies, daily, random forests, XGBoost, deep nets; claims substantial statistical and economic value (pre-2022 sample; abstract only). — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3914414)
- Li et al., Pacific-Basin Finance Journal (2026): 12 ML models vs 2 linear benchmarks, 8 macro variables and 17 crypto characteristics, OOS tests (abstract only). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927538X25003701)
- Chu, "Machine Forecast Disagreement in the Cryptocurrency Market" (JFQA forthcoming 2026): title only. — [JFQA PDF](https://jfqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/26316_Machine_Forecast_Disagreement.pdf)
- Cakici et al. (IRFA 2024): ML strategies remain profitable after costs but "alphas are concentrated in hard-to-trade assets and depend on extreme returns in small, illiquid, volatile coins." — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v94y2024ics1057521924001765.html)

### Inferences
- For a top-50 daily-bar spot app, ML on OHLCV features has no demonstrated net edge over the simple rules it already runs; the two places ML "works" are (a) small illiquid coins the app excludes and (b) hourly BTC with a cost-threshold filter that trades rarely, which is a turnover rule rather than a forecasting edge.
- The one-day reversal that drives XGBoost's R-squared is a signal the app has already dropped (RSI(2), fading big moves), and at 0.25-0.40% round trip a daily-rebalanced reversal cannot pay.
- Where ML could still help is as a *combiner* of the existing indicators (the CTREND construction), which is a cross-sectional ranking problem with weekly turnover, not a price forecast.

### Gaps
- No independent replication of any 2024-2026 crypto ML trading result was found; Baquero explicitly calls replication "an open need".
- Net-of-cost figures for the Pacific-Basin 2026 and Filippou et al. papers are behind paywalls.

## 10. LLM and sentiment signals: GPT-4/Claude/FinBERT on news, Reddit and X; Fear and Greed; Google Trends; data costs

### Takeaway
The only crypto return study with LLM sentiment and reported coefficients (Nguyen et al., China Finance Review International 2025) finds that zero-shot GPT-4o/GPT-3.5 sentiment from about 50 crypto influencers' tweets predicts next-day BTC returns in full-sample regressions with R-squared of 3.7% (8.2% with controls) while BERT and VADER show nothing; it has no out-of-sample test and no costs. The equity benchmark (Lopez-Lira and Tang) earns 34 bps/day gross long-short on 4,123 stocks and decays as LLM adoption rises. The Fear and Greed index has no predictive value in two 2026 studies; Google Trends predicts volume and volatility, not returns. No study demonstrates an edge from a once-a-day LLM read of headlines on crypto. X API access is now pay-per-use ($0.005 per post read), Reddit's free tier is non-commercial with manual approval, and CryptoPanic's current tiers could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- Nguyen, Chu, Ha and Nguyen, "Decoding market sentiment: the power of ChatGPT in explaining Bitcoin returns from X data", China Finance Review International (2025): tweets from about 50 crypto key opinion leaders (CoinDesk list) plus keyword tweets via Twitter API v2, 1,022 daily observations within a Jan 2018-Jun 2023 window; zero-shot ChatGPT-4o, ChatGPT-3.5 Turbo, BERT and VADER; one-day-lagged sentiment regressions: GPT-4o bullishness coefficient 0.006 (t about 4.9) without controls and 0.010 (t about 6.8) with controls, R-squared 3.7% and 8.2%; GPT-3.5 similar (R-squared 3.4%/7.6%); BERT R-squared 0.0%/2.7% and insignificant; VADER insignificant; no association between ChatGPT sentiment and FOMC announcements; a Sharpe comparison of a "Bull" signal portfolio vs buy-and-hold is described but results were not visible; full-sample only, no transaction costs. — [Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/cfri/article/doi/10.1108/CFRI-05-2024-0278/1301717/Decoding-market-sentiment-the-power-of-ChatGPT-in)
- "News sentiment analysis using ChatGPT for Bitcoin price dynamics", Journal of Big Data (2026): ChatGPT-scored Bitcoin headlines tested in- and out-of-sample for returns and volatility (abstract only; page redirected to login). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40537-026-01392-x)
- Ray, Mim and Logofatu (Springer chapters, 2025/2026): fine-tuned GPT-4 gives the most balanced crypto-headline sentiment classification vs FinBERT, BERT, Flan-T5, Gemma-7B; classification accuracy only, no return test. — [Springer 2026](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-10202-7_32); [Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-96235-6_23)
- arXiv 2409.02836 (GPT-4o few-shot labels on crypto tweets, five coins): investor-expectation study, not a return-prediction test. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02836)
- Lopez-Lira and Tang, "Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements?" (JFE 2026 / arXiv 2304.07619 v6, Oct 2025): 4,123 US stocks, Oct 2021-May 2024 (post-training window); GPT-4 headline scores give 34 bps/day before costs in a daily-rebalanced long-short; the initial day-0 reaction has about 90% hit rates and is not tradable at the close, the drift is the tradable part; "strategy returns decline as LLM adoption rises". Not crypto. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X26001066); [arXiv v6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619v6)
- Gurgul et al. (2025), per the Baquero review: sentiment-augmented daily models show consistent profitability and Sharpe gains, but against buy-and-hold rather than a naive forecast. — [arXiv 2606.00071](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00071v1)
- Fear and Greed index: 2018-2025 VAR study finds no Granger causality from index changes to returns and no OOS gain, returns lead sentiment; 2018-2026 study (2,945 days) finds Extreme Fear persists 82% of days, forward-return effect with 7-day CI including zero, and expanding-window forecasts that do not beat the historical mean. ### Inferences
- The LLM-sentiment evidence that exists is daily, in-sample, built on influencer tweets rather than headlines, and reports explanatory R-squared rather than a costed trading result; the equity analogue shows most of the move happens on day 0 and the edge decays with adoption. A once-a-day read of headlines at the daily close by the in-app Claude analyst has no demonstrated return edge in any crypto study found.
- The more defensible use of the in-app analyst is as a *risk* input (flag scheduled macro events, exchange/legal news that invalidates a position) rather than as a return signal; the FOMC/CPI volatility evidence in Q5 supports event-aware sizing.
- Data cost is now the binding constraint for X-based signals: at $0.005 per read, a daily sweep of 2,000 posts is $10/day, $300/month, more than a retail account's plausible edge; Reddit requires manual approval for anything resembling production use.

### Gaps

## 11. Volatility breakout rules on daily bars (open + k x range, Bollinger squeeze, ATR expansion) tested on crypto 2023-2026 net of costs

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed or code-published out-of-sample test of Larry-Williams-style range breakouts, Bollinger squeezes or ATR-expansion entries on daily crypto bars for 2023-2026 was found. The only costed results are vendor backtests: a daily long-only Bollinger-band breakout on BTC 2022-mid-2026 at 0.1% fee lost 14.2% with a 25.6% win rate and 41.9% drawdown, and a 20-day-high breakout with a 1.5xATR trailing stop reported +612.7% on five trades with an 81% drawdown. The published crypto evidence that does touch these ideas (CTREND's top features boll_mid, CCI and MACD; Quantpedia's x-day-high MAX rule holding up out of sample) supports trend confirmation, not volatility-compression entries.

### Cited Findings
- CoinQuant daily BTC Bollinger-band breakout, 2022 to mid-2026, long-only, 0.1% fee: total return -14.20%, win rate 25.6%, max drawdown 41.85%; the page compares with a buy-and-hold figure of about +22.8% over the same window (that benchmark looks low for BTC 2022-2026, so treat the page's numbers with caution). — [CoinQuant](https://www.coinquant.ai/blog/volatility-trading-strategy-on-crypto-does-it-work-data-backed)
- CoinQuant daily BTC 20-bar high breakout with 1.5xATR(14) trailing stop: +612.7%, max drawdown 81.02%, win rate 20.0%, five trades; fees not stated. — [CoinQuant](https://www.coinquant.ai/strategies/btc-atr-1d-backtest)
- StratBase table lists a BB-squeeze breakout on BTC/USDT 4h with profit factor 1.61, no fee treatment or methodology. — [StratBase](https://stratbase.ai/en/blog/bollinger-bands-strategy-guide)
- Blogs propose filters (band width above its 20-period average, volume confirmation) with no OOS test. — [Bitsgap](https://bitsgap.com/blog/best-indicators-for-identifying-crypto-breakouts)
- CTREND (JFQA 2025) variable importance: boll_mid, cci and macd score highest among the 28 indicators, i.e. Bollinger information enters as a trend-location feature inside a cross-sectional ranking, not as a squeeze-breakout entry. — [Fieberg et al., JFQA 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/trend-factor-for-the-cross-section-of-cryptocurrency-returns/4C1509ACBA33D5DCAF0AC24379148178)
- Quantpedia MAX rule (buy on an x-day high) remained effective out of sample Feb 2022-Aug 2024 on BTC, without costs. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/revisiting-trend-following-and-mean-reversion-strategies-in-bitcoin/)
- "Explainable Patterns in Cryptocurrency Microstructure" (arXiv 2602.00776) and "The Quarter-Hour Effect: Periodic Algorithmic Trading and Return Predictability in Cryptocurrency Futures" (arXiv 2607.09426) exist; the latter is sub-hourly and futures-based, so outside the app's settled scope. — [arXiv 2602.00776](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00776); [arXiv 2607.09426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09426)

### Inferences
- The app's existing Donchian breakouts already cover the one volatility-breakout family with out-of-sample support (x-day highs). Adding an open-plus-k-range or squeeze entry would be adding an untested variant whose only costed public backtest is negative.
- If a volatility-expansion idea is tried, the literature suggests using ATR/band width as a *sizing or regime* input (Q4) rather than as a trigger.

### Gaps
- No academic or code-published test of Larry Williams range breakouts on daily crypto bars was found at all.
- No multi-coin, walk-forward, costed Bollinger-squeeze test exists in the sources found.

## 12. Ranking of signals and techniques by credibility of the net evidence for long-only liquid Binance spot, daily to multi-week

Criteria: peer review or open code; net-of-cost reporting; a liquid-coin subset; a long leg reported; a post-2022 sample; independent confirmation; number of variants disclosed.

1. Volatility scaling / volatility targeting of existing trend and momentum calls. Two independent crypto backtests (one OOS 2022-24 with a bootstrap, net of fees, slippage and funding; one 2017-25 spot with 1-4 bps), consistent with the multi-asset literature. Drawdown improvement is unambiguous (-35% to -16%; -80% to -15%); Sharpe improvement is large in one test (0.65 to 1.83) and small in the other (0.71 to 0.79); net return effect is sample-dependent. Long-only compatible, low fee cost. Sources: arXiv 2602.11708; Summitward.
2. Cross-sectional multi-indicator trend composite (CTREND-style ranking of several moving-average, MACD, Bollinger, CCI, volume features). Peer-reviewed (JFQA 2025), >3,000 coins, weekly, long leg reported, top-100 subset reported, net of 30-50 bps with break-even 1.56-2.83% per trade for the long leg. Weaknesses: sample ends May 2022; second-half long leg 1.56%/week not significant alone; market beta about 1; LTW alpha of the long leg marginal; 63-67% weekly turnover (the app's 0.20% per side is within the break-even but far from free); 55,296 design variants explored by the authors (79% give a significant Sharpe). Single research group.
3. 200-day BTC gate used for sizing rather than blocking. One costed backtest (Sharpe 0.89, DD -10.4% vs B&H 1.13, DD -83.8%) plus the multi-asset finding that binary in/out is the worst signal shape. Already in the app as the SMA(50) timer family; evidence says it is a drawdown tool.
4. Low-volatility / low-beta tilt within liquid coins. Three FRL papers; the two recent ones (2021/22 liquid-coin study, 2026 post-2017 study) find the premium in the long low-vol leg; 2-3 month formation, 1-month hold; no net figures, no BTC benchmark. Single-field, abstract-only evidence.
5. Macro-event (FOMC/CPI) volatility awareness for sizing. Robust abnormal volatility (0.8-1.0 pp, p<0.001) in a 2020-2025 event study and a 2024 high-frequency paper; no directional return. Evidence supports reducing size, not entering.
6. Dollar-index / risk-off filter. One peer-reviewed real-time OOS study (Arain-Snudden 2025) finds the USD index among 2 of 12 predictors with trading value, only in large swings. Single source; consistent with the Nasdaq-correlation narrative but untested as a rule.
7. Cointegration pairs vs BTC (needs futures short leg). One peer-reviewed study, hourly, net Sharpe about 0.95 over 2021-23 at 0.04% taker; 5-minute version negative; no 2024-26 sample. Not implementable on spot at 0.40% round trip.
8. LLM sentiment from X/news. One crypto paper with coefficients (in-sample, R-squared 4-8%, no costs, no OOS); equity analogue decays with adoption; data cost now $0.005 per post read on X. No demonstrated once-a-day headline edge.
9. Seasonality (day-of-week, turn-of-month, Uptober). Recent academic work finds effects in variance/skewness not means; day-of-week results unstable across samples; Uptober is n=13 with no test and reverses by December. None reported at a size that clears 0.25-0.40% round trip on a daily hold.
10. Machine learning on OHLCV/technical features. Mostly negative replication record; OLS at least as good; daily edges are one-day reversal that cannot pay fees; positive results live in small illiquid coins.
11. Fear and Greed index, Google Trends. No predictive value for returns in 2026 studies; returns lead sentiment; search volume predicts volatility/volume.
12. Grid and DCA bots. Zero expected profit before fees by construction; negative after; dynamic variants beat buy-and-hold only in rising, volatile samples; no audited vendor data.
13. Volatility-compression breakouts (Bollinger squeeze, open + k x range, ATR expansion). No academic test; only costed public backtest is negative; the one working breakout family (x-day highs) is already in the app.
14. Triangular and cross-exchange arbitrage. Peer-reviewed 2025 evidence that it is not exploitable at retail fees and latency (96.9% of opportunities unprofitable even at VIP 9; 146 ms execution threshold; $12-18 per week on $4k).
15. BTC dominance, ETH/BTC trend, altcoin-season index as regime filters. No tested evidence found anywhere; definitions and current readings only.

New evidence touching the app's already-settled list: none found that contradicts dropping candlestick patterns, sub-hourly rules, hour-of-day tilts, RSI(2) or fading big moves; the Quantpedia 21:00-23:00 UTC intraday effect drew down in 2022-23, and the one-day reversal that drives ML R-squared confirms it exists but cannot pay spot fees. For the kept strategies, CTREND reports that most crypto momentum signals lose significance at 2-3 week holds while its multi-indicator composite stays significant to 4 weeks, which is mild support for the app's 1-4 week momentum horizons and Donchian/SMA family, with the caveat that all of that evidence ends in May 2022.
